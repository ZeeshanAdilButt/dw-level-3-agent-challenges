# Clause 8: Liability

## Purpose and Scope

This clause sets out how liability is allocated between Quillon and the customer under the Quillon contract management software agreement (the "Agreement"). It applies to all claims arising under or in connection with the Agreement, whether in contract, tort (including negligence), breach of statutory duty or otherwise. Capitalised terms not defined in this clause have the meanings given in the main body of the Agreement. This clause takes effect from 1 July 2026 and applies to all Agreements entered into or renewed on or after that date.

## 8.1 General Principle

The parties acknowledge that the fees payable under the Agreement reflect the allocation of risk set out in this clause, and that each party relies on the limits described below when entering into the Agreement. Nothing in this clause shall be read as an attempt to limit liability that cannot be limited under applicable law. The limits in clauses 8.2 and 8.3 are to be applied in the order set out below, and the more specific limit in clause 8.3 prevails over clause 8.2 where both could apply to the same claim.

The following steps describe how the liability limits operate in practice:

1. Identify the claim and confirm whether it arises from a breach of the Agreement, a negligent act or omission, or another cause of action.
2. Determine whether the claim concerns a personal data breach, meaning a breach affecting personal data processed by Quillon on the customer's behalf under the data processing terms of the Agreement.
3. If the claim concerns a personal data breach, apply the data protection cap in clause 8.3.
4. If the claim does not concern a personal data breach, apply the standard cap in clause 8.2.
5. Check whether the exclusions in clause 8.4 apply, in which case the relevant cap does not limit liability for that claim.
6. Calculate the amount of any recoverable loss, subject to the applicable cap, and no further.

## 8.2 Standard Cap

Subject to clause 8.4, each party's total liability to the other party arising under or in connection with the Agreement, whether in contract, tort (including negligence), breach of statutory duty or otherwise, shall not exceed the total fees paid in the 12 months before the event giving rise to the claim. Where the claim arises in the first 12 months of the Agreement, the cap is calculated by reference to the fees paid up to the date of the event giving rise to the claim.

The following steps apply when calculating the standard cap:

1. Identify the date of the event giving rise to the claim.
2. Total all fees actually paid by the customer under the Agreement in the 12 months before that date.
3. Use that total as the maximum aggregate liability of the relevant party for all claims arising from that event and from related events.
4. Where no fees have been paid in that period, the cap is nil, subject to clause 8.4.

The standard cap is an aggregate cap. It applies to all claims together and not separately to each claim, invoice or contract year.

## 8.3 Data Protection Cap

Subject to clause 8.4, each party's total liability to the other party for losses arising from a breach of its obligations under the data protection terms of the Agreement, including any breach of applicable data protection law in relation to personal data processed under the Agreement, shall not exceed 2 times the fees paid in the 12 months before the event giving rise to the claim.

The following steps apply when calculating the data protection cap:

1. Confirm that the claim concerns a breach of the data protection terms or of applicable data protection law in relation to personal data processed under the Agreement.
2. Identify the date of the event giving rise to the claim.
3. Total all fees actually paid by the customer under the Agreement in the 12 months before that date.
4. Multiply that total by 2 to determine the maximum aggregate liability for data protection breaches.
5. Where a claim involves both a data protection breach and another breach of the Agreement, apply the data protection cap to the data protection element and the standard cap in clause 8.2 to the remaining element, without double counting any loss.

The data protection cap is separate from the standard cap in clause 8.2. Amounts paid under the data protection cap do not reduce the amount available under the standard cap, and amounts paid under the standard cap do not reduce the amount available under the data protection cap, except where the same loss is recovered under both, in which case it may be recovered only once.

## 8.4 Exclusions from the Caps

Neither the standard cap in clause 8.2 nor the data protection cap in clause 8.3 applies to liability for:

1. fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation by a party or its personnel;
2. death or personal injury caused by negligence of a party or its personnel; or
3. any other liability that cannot be limited or excluded under applicable law.

The following steps apply where an exclusion is claimed:

1. Identify whether the claim concerns fraud, or death or personal injury caused by negligence.
2. Where it does, the claimant may recover the full amount of proven loss, without regard to the caps in clauses 8.2 and 8.3.
3. Where a claim combines an excluded head of loss with other losses, only the excluded head of loss is outside the caps, and the remaining losses remain subject to the applicable cap.

## 8.5 Exclusion of Indirect Loss

Subject to clause 8.4, neither party shall be liable to the other for any loss of profits, loss of business, loss of goodwill, or any indirect or consequential loss arising under or in connection with the Agreement, whether in contract, tort (including negligence), breach of statutory duty or otherwise.

## 8.6 Customer Responsibility

The customer is responsible for the accuracy and completeness of the contracts, templates, clauses and other content it uploads or creates within the service, and for legal advice on the content of those documents. Quillon is not liable for any loss arising from the customer's reliance on the software as a substitute for legal or professional advice.

## 8.7 Mitigation

Each party shall take reasonable steps to mitigate any loss it suffers arising from an event for which the other party may be liable. Any amount recoverable under this clause shall be reduced to the extent that the loss results from the claiming party's failure to mitigate.
