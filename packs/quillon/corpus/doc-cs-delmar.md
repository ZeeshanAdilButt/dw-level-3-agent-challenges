# Delmar Treasury: customer story

Delmar Treasury is a corporate finance team that manages supplier agreements, vendor terms and payment commitments across several business units. Like many finance groups, it found that its contracts lived in shared drives, email threads and spreadsheets, and that approvals moved slowly because no one could tell at a glance where a document stood. This story describes how the team moved its contract work onto Quillon, what it configured, and what changed.

## The problem before Quillon

Before adopting Quillon, the treasury team handled each new agreement by hand. Drafts started from whichever prior version someone could find, clause wording drifted from one document to the next, and approvals were chased through forwarded emails. Finance leads often waited several working days for sign-off before a contract could be countersigned, and renewal dates were tracked in a spreadsheet that was rarely up to date. Missed renewals were a recurring concern because a lapsed supplier term could leave the company paying above-market rates or without coverage.

The team also struggled to answer simple questions quickly. When an auditor or a business unit lead asked which agreements contained a particular termination clause, the answer required opening files one at a time.

## What the team set up

Delmar Treasury works on the Pro plan. The team began by connecting its ERP system, NetSuite, to Quillon. The integration was completed in under 2 hours, with the finance operations lead handling the configuration and a Quillon onboarding specialist answering questions during the session. Supplier records and payment terms were then linked to the contracts they governed, so that a contract's financial details appeared alongside its legal text.

Next, the team built a standard clause library covering payment terms, liability limits, confidentiality and termination. Each approved clause was stored once and reused in drafts, which removed much of the variation that had previously crept into supplier agreements. Templates were created for the four contract types the team handles most often, and each template routed to the correct approval chain automatically.

The team then configured approvals so that a contract moved to the next reviewer as soon as the previous one signed off, with reminders sent to anyone whose step was overdue. Signatures were collected through Quillon's e-signature feature. Obligation tracking was set up for payment milestones and reporting duties, and renewal alerts were scheduled well ahead of each notice deadline. Finally, the team used the cross-contract search to build a single view of every active agreement.

## Measured results

The change that mattered most to the team was speed. Approval time fell from 5 days to 1 day for standard supplier agreements, because reviewers now receive a clear task instead of a forwarded email and can see the full contract history in one place.

Two other outcomes were noted in the team's review. Renewal alerts replaced the manual spreadsheet, so no notice deadline has been left to memory, and search across signed contracts now answers portfolio questions in minutes rather than hours.

## In their words

"Our approvals used to stall in people's inboxes, and we had no reliable way to know which renewals were coming up," said the finance operations lead at Delmar Treasury. "Now every contract has one owner, one clear next step and one set of dates we can trust. The NetSuite connection was the piece we were most worried about, and it was up and running the same day we started."
