# Renewal alerts

Renewal alerts help your team act on contracts before they renew automatically. Instead of finding out about a renewal after the notice window has closed, the right people hear about it early enough to negotiate, amend, or cancel.

## How alerts work

Every contract with a renewal date in Quillon can trigger alerts. By default, renewal alerts fire 90, 60 and 30 days before the renewal date. These defaults give most teams enough time to review pricing, check usage, and decide whether to renew on current terms.

You can change the timing for an individual contract when its notice period is different from the default, for example when a vendor requires 120 days of written notice to cancel.

## Who receives alerts

Renewal alerts go to the contract owner and one backup person. Naming a backup means the alert still reaches someone when the owner is on leave, has changed roles, or has left the company. Quillon records both people on the contract record, so you can update them at any time.

## Example

A finance team tracks a three-year software license with an automatic renewal. The contract owner receives the first alert 90 days before renewal, and the backup receives the same alert. At the 60-day alert, the owner opens the contract in Quillon, reviews the obligation tracker for usage terms, and starts a renegotiation. The 30-day alert serves as a final reminder if no decision has been recorded.

## Managing alerts

Alerts appear in your notification center and can also be sent by email. Each alert links directly to the contract, so you can review the clause, check the notice period, and record a decision in one place.
