# Support runbook: refunds and access

Internal playbook for Quillon support leads. Effective from 2026-08-01. Staff only. Do not share this document with customers.

## Purpose and scope

This runbook explains how support leads handle refund requests and access issues for customers using Quillon's contract-management platform, which covers drafting from templates, the clause library, approvals, e-signature, obligation tracking, renewal alerts, and search across signed contracts. It is a guide, so use your judgment within the limits below, and escalate when a case falls outside them.

## Refund authority

Support leads can approve refunds of up to $500 without finance. Work through the request, confirm the charge and the reason for the refund, and record the approval in the ticket before the refund is processed.

Refunds above $500 need finance approval. Do not promise a refund amount to the customer until finance has signed off. Open a finance review in the ticket, attach the billing history and the reason for the request, and tell the customer that the request is under review and that you will update them once a decision is made.

When a request is split across several charges, treat the total as the amount being refunded. A set of small refunds that together exceed $500 still requires finance approval.

## Steps for a refund request

1. Confirm the account owner or an authorized administrator is making the request.
2. Check the billing history and identify the charge in question.
3. Record the reason for the refund in the ticket.
4. If the amount is $500 or less, approve and process the refund, then note the approval in the ticket.
5. If the amount is above $500, submit the request for finance approval and wait for a decision before replying with an outcome.
6. Close the ticket only after the customer has been told the result.

## Access and impersonation

Support staff may impersonate a user only with a signed ticket and the customer's consent. The signed ticket must be on file before any session starts, and it must name the user being impersonated and the reason for the session. Consent must come from the customer, and a verbal statement from a colleague does not count.

Do not impersonate a user to resolve a refund question, to view contract content the user has not asked you to review, or to bypass an approval step in the platform. If the customer withdraws consent during a session, end it immediately and note the time in the ticket.

## Escalation

Escalate to a senior support lead when a refund request involves a disputed contract, when a customer reports data exposure, or when you are unsure whether a request is within your authority. Escalate to finance for any refund above $500. Record every escalation in the ticket with a short summary so the next person does not need to ask the customer again.

## Records

Keep the approval, the reason, the customer's consent (for access work), and the final outcome in the ticket. Tickets are the audit record for refunds and access, so do not handle either outside the ticketing system.
