# Pemberton & Vale: customer story

Pemberton & Vale is a mid-sized law firm that handles commercial leases, supplier agreements, and employment contracts for a range of clients. Like many firms of its size, it had built up years of contracts across shared drives, email attachments, and a few paper binders, which made every new matter a search through old files. This customer story describes how the firm moved its contract work onto Quillon, what it set up, and what changed.

## The problem before Quillon

Before adopting Quillon, the firm's drafting process depended on copying old documents and editing them by hand. Associates kept private collections of preferred clauses, and senior partners often could not tell which version of a standard term was current. Approvals ran through email chains, so it was common for a redline to be sent to the wrong reviewer or for a final version to go out without the required sign-off. Once a contract was signed, the firm rarely tracked its obligations, such as notice periods or renewal dates, until a client called to ask.

The firm's operations lead described the situation this way:

> "We were spending more time finding the right document than writing it. Renewal dates were tracked in someone's head, and that is not a system you can trust when clients are depending on you."

## What the firm set up

Pemberton & Vale is on the Starter plan, and its rollout focused on the parts of the platform that matched its daily work. The team began by building a set of standard templates for its most common agreement types, then loaded the approved clauses into the clause library so that associates could insert vetted language directly into drafts. Each template was linked to an approval route, so that a draft moved to the right reviewers automatically rather than through ad hoc emails.

The firm enabled e-signature for routine client agreements, which removed the need to print, scan, and reconcile signed copies. It also set up obligation tracking and renewal alerts for its signed contracts, so that deadlines appeared in front of the responsible lawyer ahead of time. Finally, the team ran search across its signed contracts, which brought its archive of older agreements into one place.

The partner responsible for the rollout noted that setup was straightforward. Pemberton & Vale went live in one afternoon, with the team importing existing templates and inviting users during a single working session.

## Measured results

The firm measured its results against its own drafting and approval workflow after the first months of use. Pemberton & Vale drafts contracts 4 times faster than it did before adopting Quillon. Approvals now move through defined routes, and the firm reports fewer documents going to the wrong reviewer.

Renewal and obligation tracking has also changed how the firm works with clients. Deadlines that once depended on memory now appear in a shared view, and the firm can answer questions about a signed agreement by searching the archive rather than reopening old files.

## Looking ahead

Pemberton & Vale plans to extend its clause library as new practice areas grow and to keep its templates current as its precedent bank matures. The firm's experience reflects a broader lesson: a contract system is most valuable when it matches how the team already works, and when the most common tasks are fast to set up and easy to repeat.
