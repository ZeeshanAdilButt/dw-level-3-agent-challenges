# Ironbridge Chambers: customer story

Ironbridge Chambers is a commercial law practice that advises businesses on contracts, disputes and regulatory matters. Its lawyers handle a steady flow of agreements, from supplier terms and employment documents to commercial leases and financing arrangements. For a firm of this kind, the quality of its contract work depends on how quickly the team can find the right precedent, the right clause and the right signed document when a client needs an answer.

This customer story describes how Ironbridge Chambers moved its contract work onto Quillon, what the firm set up, and what changed afterwards. It is written from interviews with the firm's knowledge management lead and with several fee earners who use the system every day. The firm subscribes to Quillon's Pro plan.

## The problem before Quillon

Before adopting Quillon, Ironbridge Chambers kept its contracts in a combination of shared drives, email attachments and a legacy document system that had been added to over many years. Templates lived in different folders depending on which practice group had created them, and clauses were copied from one matter to the next, often with small edits that were never recorded. When a partner asked whether the firm had previously agreed a particular indemnity wording, the honest answer was frequently that someone would have to look.

Finding signed agreements was the most painful part of the process. Fee earners described opening several folders, searching by client name and then reading through documents to confirm whether a version was the final signed copy. The firm's knowledge management lead estimated that a typical search for a specific clause or signed agreement took around twenty-five minutes, and that the time was spent almost entirely on locating material rather than on the legal judgment that followed.

Obligations were also tracked informally. Renewal dates, notice periods and reporting duties were recorded in individual calendars or in spreadsheets maintained by different teams. Missed notice dates were rare but not unknown, and the firm wanted a single place where the commitments in its clients' agreements were visible.

## What Ironbridge Chambers set up

The firm began its move to Quillon by agreeing a set of ground rules with its practice group leads. Each group nominated a senior lawyer to review its templates before they were loaded, so that the standard wording used across the firm reflected current practice rather than a collection of historic versions. The firm then built a clause library organised by subject and by risk position, with approved fallback wording attached to each entry so that junior lawyers could see what the firm considered acceptable without asking for permission every time.

Drafting from templates was the first feature the firm rolled out. Fee earners began creating new agreements from approved templates and inserting clauses from the library, rather than starting from an old matter file. Approvals were configured so that a draft leaving the team for a client required sign-off from a supervising lawyer, and the approval history was kept with the document.

E-signature was connected to the same workflow, so that a finalised agreement could be sent to the counterparty and returned without leaving the system. Once signed, the executed copy was stored in the repository with its metadata intact.

The most significant decision concerned the historic archive. Ironbridge Chambers chose to bring its existing signed contracts into Quillon in a single structured migration rather than leaving them in the legacy system. The team prepared the import by removing duplicates, confirming which copies were executed, and tagging each document with its client, matter and practice area. That preparation took the firm's knowledge management team several weeks, and it was the reason the search function became useful so quickly. Obligation tracking and renewal alerts were then set up for the agreements that carried ongoing commitments, so that the firm could see upcoming notice dates across its client base in one view.

## The rollout

The firm did not switch every team at once. It started with the commercial contracts group, which handled the highest volume of agreements and had the clearest need for consistent templates. That group worked through a pilot period in which its members used the new system for all new matters while continuing to consult the legacy archive for older files. Feedback from the pilot led to changes in how clauses were tagged, because early users found that some categories were too broad to be useful, and the team refined the taxonomy before extending the system further.

Training was delivered in short sessions tied to real matters rather than as a general product introduction. The knowledge management lead ran sessions in which lawyers brought a live piece of work and learned to find precedents, insert library clauses and set up an obligation for it. This approach made adoption faster in the employment and real estate groups, which came onto the system after the commercial group had shaken out the early issues.

Some parts of the rollout required more effort than the firm expected. Older documents were not always clearly marked as executed, and checking them took longer than planned. Certain templates also needed to be split into jurisdiction-specific versions. The firm treated these as normal parts of a migration of this size and resolved them through the review process it had already set up.

## What changed

The firm now measures its search performance as part of its regular review of the system. Searches that once took around twenty-five minutes now take a fraction of that time. For a fee earner looking for a previously agreed indemnity or a signed supplier agreement, the answer is typically available within a few moments of entering the search. Partners report that they more often ask for precedent before drafting and less often have to reconstruct a position from memory.

The firm has also seen an improvement in how it manages obligations. Renewal and notice dates are visible in one place, and the alerts give the responsible team time to act before a deadline falls. The knowledge management lead noted that the most valuable change was not any single feature but the confidence that the firm's contract record was complete and searchable.

Measured against the firm's own baseline, the results of the migration are as follows. Ironbridge Chambers cut search time from 25 minutes to under 2 minutes. The firm indexed 52,000 documents as part of the migration, so the search results now cover the full range of its signed contract archive rather than only the material that had been kept in order.

## In the words of the team

The firm's knowledge management lead, who led the project, said the change was felt most by lawyers who had previously spent the most time looking for material. "We used to measure how long people spent hunting for an old agreement, and it was a surprising share of their week," the lead said. "Now a fee earner can find the precedent and get back to the advice, which is the work our clients are paying for."

A senior associate in the commercial group said the clause library had changed how the team drafted. "The fallback wording is what I rely on most. I can see what the firm is comfortable with and why, and I'm not reinventing a position on every matter."

## Looking ahead

Ironbridge Chambers intends to extend the system to its remaining practice groups over the coming year and to expand its use of obligation tracking as more agreements are added. The firm has said it will continue to review its templates and clause library with its practice leads, so that the material in the system keeps pace with changes in its clients' businesses and in the law.

For other firms considering a similar move, the firm's advice is to invest early in the quality of the archive and the templates. The software, it says, works well only when the material it holds has been checked, tagged and agreed by the lawyers who will rely on it.
