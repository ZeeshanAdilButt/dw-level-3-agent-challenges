# Northcote LLP: customer story

Northcote LLP is a commercial law firm whose practice spans corporate transactions, employment matters, and property work. Like many firms of its size, Northcote had built up a large body of contracts over the years, held in a patchwork of shared drives, email attachments, and filing cabinets. This customer story describes how the firm moved that body of work onto Quillon, which firm staff now use for drafting, approvals, signatures, and ongoing obligation tracking. It is published with the agreement of the firm and reflects the firm's own account of the project.

## The problem before

For most of its history, Northcote managed contracts through a combination of habit and goodwill. Templates lived in folders that different teams had copied and edited over time, so two people drafting the same type of agreement could easily end up working from different versions. Intake was equally informal. A new matter might arrive with a client's own paper, a partner's marked-up draft, or a scanned signature page, and the people responsible for logging it had to re-key the key terms by hand into a spreadsheet.

The firm's operations lead described the difficulty plainly. "We were not short of good lawyers or good work," she said. "We were short of a single place where you could trust that the version in front of you was the right one, and where a renewal date you had been relying on would not quietly slip past."

Those gaps had practical consequences. Intake errors, such as a wrong counterparty name, a missed notice period, or an incorrect payment term copied from the wrong source, were common enough that the firm had built a manual second-check step, which itself added delay. Renewals were tracked in individual calendars, so an important date depended on one person remembering to look at the right file at the right time. Searching across old agreements meant opening them one by one.

## What Northcote set up

Northcote chose Quillon on the Pro plan and began by treating the migration as an opportunity to rationalise rather than a simple copy of existing files. The firm's knowledge management team first agreed a short list of its most-used agreement types, then rebuilt the templates for those types inside Quillon using the clause library. Approved clauses were stored once and reused across templates, so a change to a limitation of liability wording could be reviewed and adopted in one place rather than hunted down across dozens of documents.

The approval workflow was set up to match how partners already worked. Drafts move from associate to supervising partner to, where required, a risk or compliance reviewer, and each step records who approved what and when. The firm kept its existing signing practice but moved it into Quillon's e-signature function, so that the signed version is stored against the matter it belongs to rather than sitting in an inbox.

Obligation tracking was the feature the team spent the most time configuring. For each signed agreement, the key dates and obligations, such as notice periods, reporting deadlines, and renewal windows, were recorded as structured fields. Renewal alerts were then routed to the responsible matter lead with enough lead time to act. The firm also made search across signed contracts a standard part of its intake process, so that a new matter could be checked against existing agreements with the same counterparty before work began.

## The rollout

The migration itself was the largest single piece of work. Northcote's team had to decide what to bring across, what to archive, and what to leave behind. The firm chose to migrate its active and recently expired agreements in full, and to keep older material in its existing archive with a reference index in Quillon. Working in batches by practice group allowed each group to check its own documents, catch mislabelled counterparties, and confirm that key dates had carried across correctly.

The early weeks were not without friction. Some fee-earners were used to working in their own folders and resisted moving their drafts into a shared system, and a few template variants turned out to be near-duplicates that needed reconciling before they could be retired. The knowledge management team responded by holding short working sessions with each practice group rather than issuing a general instruction, and by keeping a visible log of decisions so that people could see why a template had been merged or a clause had been rewritten.

By the end of the migration, the firm had moved 18,000 contracts in 6 weeks, a pace the operations lead described as ambitious but manageable because the work was broken into groups with clear owners. Once the bulk of the material was in place, the focus shifted to daily use: making sure drafting always started from the approved template, that approvals were routed correctly, and that renewal alerts were reaching the right people. The firm ran a parallel check for the first month, comparing new intake records against the old spreadsheets to confirm that nothing important was being lost in translation.

## The measured results

Northcote measured the change against its own intake process before and after the rollout. The clearest result is in the accuracy of new matter intake. Northcote cut intake errors by 64%, which the firm attributes mainly to structured fields replacing manual re-keying and to searchable templates replacing copied files. The manual second-check step is still used for high-value matters, but the firm reports that it now catches far fewer issues and has been scaled back for routine work.

The firm also reports that renewals are no longer dependent on individual memory. Alerts are visible to the matter lead and to the operations team, and missed renewal dates have become rare enough that the firm no longer treats them as a standing risk to review each month. Search across signed contracts has, in the firm's words, turned a half-day task into a few minutes' work when a partner needs to check what has been agreed with a particular counterparty.

## In their words

Asked what they would tell another firm considering a similar move, the operations lead gave a measured answer. "Do the template and clause work before you migrate, not after. If you move a mess into a new system, you just have a tidier mess. Once the templates were right, everything else followed more easily, and the people doing the work could see the benefit within weeks rather than months."

Northcote continues to refine its setup, and the firm's knowledge management team is now reviewing its less common agreement types to decide which should be added to the template library next.
