# Battlecard: Pactline

## Purpose

This battlecard equips the sales team to position Quillon against Pactline in competitive deals with law firms and corporate finance teams. It is for internal use only and must never be shared with customers or prospects.

## Competitive Overview

Pactline is a contract-management competitor in the same buyer segments we serve. Its comparable plan is listed at $44 per seat per month, so price comparisons should be made on total cost of ownership, including the features each platform includes in that seat price.

## Where Pactline Is Weaker

Pactline has no native obligation tracking, so buyers who need to monitor deliverables, payment milestones and compliance duties after signature will have to rely on workarounds. Quillon includes obligation tracking as part of the core product, and sales should draw this contrast clearly when the prospect's post-signature workflow matters.

## What Usually Wins the Deal

Deals against Pactline are usually won on renewal alerts. Renewal alerts help customers avoid missed notice windows and unwanted auto-renewals, which is a concrete risk for finance and legal teams. Lead with a live demonstration of how alerts surface upcoming renewals, and tie the value to avoided costs.

## Positioning Guidance

Frame Quillon as the end-to-end platform covering drafting from templates, clause library, approvals, e-signature, obligation tracking, renewal alerts and search across signed contracts. Avoid disparaging the competitor by name in written materials, and verify any claim about competitor features against current information before using it in a call.

## Handling Objections

If the prospect focuses on seat price, redirect to the full feature set and the cost of manual tracking. If the prospect says they already track obligations elsewhere, ask how that process holds up at renewal time and when deadlines fall.
