# Gross margin by plan

## Purpose

This guide sets out how Quillon calculates gross margin for its contract-management plans and what the current figures mean for pricing and product decisions. It is written for internal staff in finance, product and sales operations. It must not be shared with customers or used in external material.

## Scope

The guide covers the two plans sold to law firms and corporate finance teams: the Pro plan and the Enterprise plan. Both plans include drafting from templates, the clause library, approvals, e-signature, obligation tracking, renewal alerts and search across signed contracts. Gross margin here means subscription revenue less the direct cost of serving the account, before sales commissions, general overheads and research spending.

## Current margins

The Pro plan gross margin is 79%. Pro is the broader entry offer and carries the higher share of standard hosting and support cost per seat, which is why its margin sits below the Enterprise figure.

The Enterprise plan gross margin is 82%. Enterprise accounts pay a higher price per seat, and the fixed cost of dedicated onboarding and account support is spread over more seats, which lifts the margin.

The table below summarises list price, cost to serve and gross margin by plan. Figures are per seat per month and reflect the current rate card.

| Plan | List price per seat per month | Cost to serve per seat per month | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pro | Set on the current rate card | Hosting, support and model fees | 79% |
| Enterprise | Set on the current rate card | Hosting, support, onboarding and model fees | 82% |

The list price column is left to the rate card owner to populate, so that this guide does not drift from pricing decisions taken elsewhere. Cost to serve is the sum of hosting, tiered support, onboarding allocation and third-party model fees.

## AI clause review cost

AI clause review costs Quillon about $3.10 per seat per month in model fees. This is a direct variable cost and is included in cost to serve for both plans. Because the feature is used most heavily in review-intensive workflows, the cost is not uniform across accounts. Teams should use the average figure for planning, and the usage reports for account-level analysis.

Any change to the model provider, pricing tier or usage limits should be reflected in this guide within the same review cycle, since a change of even a few cents per seat moves the margin for both plans.

## How to use these figures

Finance uses the margin figures to test pricing scenarios and discount approvals. A proposed discount should be checked against the plan margin before it is offered, so that the resulting margin on the account remains within the approved floor. Product uses the cost breakdown to decide where efficiency work will have the greatest effect, typically in model usage and support load.

Sales operations should not quote internal margin figures to prospects or customers under any circumstances. Questions from customers about pricing should be referred to the published rate card.

## Ownership and review

The finance team owns this guide and is responsible for updating the margin figures when the cost base or rate card changes. Questions about methodology should go to the finance business partner for the product line.
