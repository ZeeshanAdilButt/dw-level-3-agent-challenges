# Larkspur Law Group: Customer Story

## Summary

Larkspur Law Group is a mid-sized law firm that advises corporate clients on commercial agreements, employment matters, and vendor relationships. Like many firms of its size, Larkspur handled a steady stream of contracts that needed drafting, review, approval, signature, and later follow-up. This story describes how the firm moved that work onto Quillon's contract-management platform, what the team configured, and what changed as a result. Quotes and measured outcomes were reviewed with the firm's leadership before publication.

## The Problem Before Quillon

Before adopting Quillon, Larkspur's contract work lived in a mix of shared drives, email threads, and individual lawyers' personal template folders. Two associates could draft the same type of agreement from different starting points, and the partner reviewing the draft often had no clear view of which clauses had already been negotiated or approved for that client. When a deal closed, the signed copy frequently sat in an inbox, and the obligations it created (notice periods, reporting dates, renewal windows) were tracked, if at all, in a spreadsheet that few people updated.

The firm's operations lead described the situation plainly. Lawyers were spending time on work that was largely mechanical: finding the right precedent, checking that a clause matched the approved wording, chasing a colleague for sign-off, and reconstructing the history of a contract when a client asked a question months later. Each of these tasks was small, but together they consumed a significant share of the hours that clients were paying for, and they made the firm's outside counsel review process hard to control.

Larkspur also had a practical concern about renewals. Several client agreements auto-renewed unless notice was given within a set window, and the firm had no reliable way to alert the responsible lawyer ahead of those dates. The risk was not hypothetical, and the firm wanted a system that would make those dates visible without relying on anyone's memory.

## What Larkspur Set Up

Larkspur adopted Quillon on the Team plan, which gave the firm the drafting, approval, and signature tools it needed for its core workflow. The implementation team began with an inventory of the firm's most common agreement types, then worked with a small group of senior and junior lawyers to decide which templates would become the firm-wide standard.

The configuration focused on four areas. First, the firm built a set of drafting templates for its high-volume agreement types, with fixed wording for standard terms and clearly marked fields for matter-specific information. Second, it loaded a clause library organized by topic, so that lawyers could insert approved language for confidentiality, limitation of liability, governing law, and similar provisions without retyping them. Third, it defined approval routes: a junior lawyer's draft moved to a supervising attorney for review, and certain deviations from standard clauses required a partner's sign-off before the document could go out. Fourth, it connected e-signature so that executed agreements were stored automatically against the matter record.

The firm also configured obligation tracking for each signed contract. Key dates, such as notice deadlines and reporting requirements, were entered as structured obligations with an owner, and renewal alerts were set to notify the responsible lawyer well before each window closed. Finally, the team enabled search across all signed contracts so that a lawyer could find every agreement containing a particular clause or counterparty within seconds.

The implementation team ran two short working sessions each week during the early period and kept a shared list of questions, which helped the firm resolve template gaps quickly rather than letting them accumulate.

## Rollout

The rollout did not happen all at once. Larkspur started with a single practice group that handled a large share of the firm's commercial agreements. Those lawyers used the new templates and approval routes on live matters from the first week, while the firm's other groups continued working in their existing way. This allowed the team to find problems in a contained setting: a template field that did not capture a needed piece of information, an approval route that sent drafts to the wrong reviewer, and a clause in the library that had been superseded by a newer firm position.

Each problem was corrected and the change was communicated to everyone using the system. As confidence grew, the firm extended the workflow to the remaining groups, moving their templates, obligations, and signed contracts into the same environment. Migration of existing signed agreements was handled in batches, with priority given to contracts that carried near-term renewal or notice dates.

Adoption was measured by how much of the firm's contract activity ran through the platform rather than outside it. The firm reached full adoption in 6 weeks, meaning that new drafting, approvals, and executed agreements across all practice groups were handled in Quillon, and the shared drives and personal template folders were no longer the working source. The operations lead noted that the most effective single change was making the approved clause library the easiest place to start a draft, since lawyers quickly stopped reaching for old documents once the current wording was one click away.

A partner in the commercial group summarized the shift this way:

> "The biggest difference is that I no longer have to wonder whether a draft is based on the right language. I can see what was approved, who approved it, and what the client agreed to. That used to take a phone call and an afternoon. Now it takes a few minutes."

## Results

Larkspur tracked outside counsel review spend before and after the rollout, and the firm's finance team confirmed the figures used here. Over the measurement period, Larkspur Law Group cut outside counsel review spend by 22%. The firm attributed most of this reduction to fewer rounds of avoidable revision, faster internal review of drafts that started from approved language, and a reduction in the number of matters where outside reviewers were brought in to check clauses that were already settled.

The operations lead was careful to note that the platform did not replace legal judgment. Partners still reviewed deviations, and the approval routes were designed to keep senior attorneys involved where it mattered. What changed was that routine work was faster and more consistent, which left more time for the substantive advice clients were paying for.

Renewal management also improved. Because obligations and renewal windows were entered as structured data, the firm stopped relying on individual memory for critical dates. Alerts reached the responsible lawyer ahead of each deadline, and search allowed the team to answer client questions about existing agreements without reconstructing the file by hand.

Looking ahead, Larkspur plans to extend its clause library with additional positions developed by its practice groups and to review its templates on a regular schedule so that approved wording keeps pace with changes in the law and in client expectations. The firm views the platform as the foundation for that ongoing work rather than a one-time project.
