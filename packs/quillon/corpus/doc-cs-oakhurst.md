# Oakhurst Payments: customer story

Oakhurst Payments is a payments company whose finance and operations teams manage a steady flow of vendor agreements, customer terms, and partner contracts. Before adopting Quillon, the finance team handled these documents through a shared drive, email threads, and a set of spreadsheets that tracked renewal dates and approval status. This story describes how the team changed that process, what they set up, and what they measured afterward.

## The problem before Quillon

The finance team's main difficulty was visibility. Contracts lived in several places, and no one could confidently answer simple questions such as which agreements were up for renewal in the next quarter or which vendor terms required a notice period. Drafting new agreements meant copying old files and editing them by hand, which led to inconsistent clauses and occasional errors that legal had to catch late in the process. New team members spent much of their first days learning where documents were kept and who needed to approve them.

Onboarding was the clearest symptom. A new hire in finance needed access to the shared drive, the approval queue, and the signature tools, and each request was handled by a different person. Getting a new team member fully working took three days on average.

## What they set up

The team began on the Team plan and built their workflow around four Quillon features. First, they created a set of standard templates for the agreements they sign most often, and drew the approved language from a clause library so that every draft started from the same vetted wording. Second, they configured approval routing so that contracts above a set value passed through the right reviewers automatically, with a clear record of each decision. Third, they turned on obligation tracking and renewal alerts, so that notice dates and renewal windows appeared in the team's calendar well before they arrived. Finally, they used search across signed contracts so that anyone could find a current agreement and its key terms without asking around.

Identity was the last piece. Oakhurst Payments signs all 20 seats in through SAML single sign-on, so the finance team uses the same company login they already use for other tools, and access is removed centrally when someone leaves the team.

## The results

The changes showed up quickly in the numbers the team tracks. Onboarding for new finance hires dropped from 3 days to 4 hours, because access and approval rights are now granted through the same configured workflow rather than a series of separate requests. Renewal dates that used to be found by manual review now surface automatically, and the team reports fewer missed notice windows since the alerts were enabled.

Drafting also became faster and more consistent, since templates and clauses removed much of the copying and re-checking that used to occur on every agreement.

## In their words

"We used to spend more time finding the right contract than negotiating it," said Maya Ferreira, Finance Operations Lead at Oakhurst Payments. "Now the templates, the approvals, and the renewal alerts all live in one place, and a new person can be productive on their first morning. It has made our whole finance function calmer."

Oakhurst Payments continues to expand its use of Quillon as additional teams begin sharing the same contract records.
