# Stonebrook Finance: customer story

## The problem before Quillon

Stonebrook Finance is a corporate finance team that manages a large volume of supplier, lending and services agreements. Before adopting Quillon, the team kept its contracts in a mix of shared drives, email attachments and a legacy document system that few people trusted. Templates were copied from old deals, clause wording drifted over time, and approvals ran through email threads that were hard to audit.

The most visible cost was storage and clutter. Expired agreements sat alongside active ones, and nobody had a clear rule for what to keep, what to archive and what to remove. When an auditor or a business lead asked for a specific signed agreement, locating it could take hours. Renewal dates were tracked in personal calendars, so some contracts lapsed without anyone deciding that they should.

## What Stonebrook set up

The finance operations group worked with Quillon's onboarding team to configure the workspace around its own agreement types. The setup covered four areas:

- **Templates and clause library.** The team consolidated its most-used agreement templates and approved clauses into a single library, so that standard wording was drawn from one source rather than copied between files.
- **Approvals and e-signature.** Drafts move through a defined approval path before they are sent for signature, and signed agreements are stored in the same record as their approvals.
- **Obligation tracking and renewal alerts.** Key deliverables and renewal dates are recorded against each contract, and the system raises alerts ahead of each date so that the business owner can decide whether to renew, renegotiate or let the agreement end.
- **Retention rules.** Stonebrook configured its workspace to archive contracts automatically after seven years, which gave the team a clear, repeatable rule for moving older records out of active storage.

The team also made search across signed contracts the default way to find an agreement, replacing the practice of opening folders one by one.

## What Stonebrook measured

Stonebrook tracked results over the first year of use. The most significant outcome was in storage: the team cut storage of expired contracts by 45%, because the automatic archiving rule moved records out of active storage on a predictable schedule instead of leaving them to accumulate.

Beyond storage, the team reported that renewal decisions were made on time more consistently, since alerts reached the responsible owner before each date rather than after. Finding a signed agreement for an audit or a business query became a search task rather than a hunt through folders.

## What the team says

"Before, our contracts lived in too many places for anyone to be confident they had the right version," said Priya Raman, Head of Finance Operations at Stonebrook Finance. "Now the template, the approvals, the signature and the renewal date sit together. The archiving rule alone changed how we think about what we keep, and the 45% reduction in expired contract storage is the number our leadership asks about most."

## Looking ahead

Stonebrook plans to extend its clause library to cover additional agreement types and to review its approval paths as the business grows. The team's experience shows how a single, governed record for each contract can reduce storage burden, make renewals predictable and make signed agreements easy to find when they are needed.
