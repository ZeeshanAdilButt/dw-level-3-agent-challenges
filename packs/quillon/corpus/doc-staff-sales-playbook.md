# Sales playbook

**Document type:** Internal playbook (guide)
**Audience:** Internal, sales and sales-adjacent staff only. Not for distribution to customers.
**Effective from:** 2026-08-01

## Purpose

This playbook describes how Quillon sales representatives move a prospect from first conversation to signed agreement. Quillon sells contract-management software to law firms and corporate finance teams. Our product covers drafting from templates, a clause library, internal approvals, e-signature, obligation tracking, renewal alerts, and search across signed contracts. Buyers in these segments are careful and skeptical, so this playbook favors accurate qualification and clear commitments over speed.

## Pipeline stages

Every opportunity sits in exactly one stage at a time. Move it forward only when the exit criteria for the current stage are met.

1. **Discovery:** We have spoken with someone who owns or influences contract work. We understand their current process and the main pain points, such as slow drafting, missed renewal dates, or difficulty finding executed agreements.
2. **Evaluation:** The prospect is actively comparing options. We have agreed on the use cases to test and, where relevant, have started a trial.
3. **Negotiation:** Commercial terms, security questionnaires, and legal review are in progress.
4. **Closed won or closed lost:** The agreement is signed, or the prospect has clearly decided not to proceed. Record a reason either way.

## Qualification

An opportunity counts as qualified once the buyer confirms a budget and a named signer. A verbal interest or an enthusiastic demo does not qualify a deal by itself. If the buyer cannot yet name who signs, keep the opportunity in Discovery or Evaluation and set a dated next step to find that person. Record the budget owner and the signer in the opportunity record before moving forward.

## Free trials

Trials help buyers test the clause library and approval workflow against real documents, and they are a valuable qualification tool. The following rules apply:

- A rep may extend a free trial once, by 7 days, without approval. Any further extension needs written approval from the sales manager.
- Trials should be tied to named use cases agreed with the buyer, and the trial owner should be recorded in the opportunity.
- Trial accounts must not be used with real client data unless the prospect has completed the required data-handling steps.

## Pricing and incentives

Reps have some room to make offers on annual agreements, and that room has a firm limit. A rep may never offer more than 1 free month on an annual deal. Any proposal beyond that requires deal desk review before it is shared with the prospect. Do not promise features, timelines, or discounts that are not in the current price list or approved in writing.

## Working the deal

Keep the opportunity record current. Each open deal needs a next step with a date. When a buyer raises a security, data residency, or contract clause concern, route it to the appropriate internal owner rather than answering from memory. Be candid about what Quillon does and does not yet do. Overpromising creates renewal risk, which is harder to fix than a lost deal.

## Handoff after signature

When a deal closes won, the account executive completes the handoff note for onboarding. The note should cover the signer, the use cases, the integrations discussed, and any commitments made during the sale.
