# Brightwater Finance: customer story

Brightwater Finance is a corporate finance team that manages a large and steadily growing portfolio of supplier agreements. Like many finance groups, it found that contracts were spread across shared drives, email attachments and signing tools, and that the people responsible for keeping suppliers to their commitments were often the last to hear about them. This story describes how the team moved its contract work onto Quillon, what it set up, and what changed.

## The problem before Quillon

Before adopting Quillon, the finance team tracked supplier obligations in a set of spreadsheets maintained by different people for different categories of spend. Renewal dates were noted in calendar entries that were easy to miss, and clauses such as service credits, notice periods and reporting duties were recorded only in the original documents. When a renewal approached, someone had to reopen the signed agreement, find the relevant terms and decide whether to renegotiate, renew or let the contract lapse. The work was reliable only when the person who knew the portfolio was available.

The team also spent considerable time creating new agreements. Each request started from an older document, which was copied, edited and sent for review by email. Versions multiplied, and it was not always clear which redline reflected the approved position.

## What Brightwater Finance set up

The team adopted Quillon on its Team plan and began by moving its existing signed agreements into a single searchable library. Once the library was in place, the team built a small set of standard templates for its most common supplier agreements and added a clause library holding the approved wording for payment terms, liability limits, data handling and termination. Drafters now build new agreements from these templates and pick clauses from the library rather than copying from prior deals.

For approvals, the team configured review steps that match its internal delegation rules, so that agreements above certain thresholds pass through the right reviewers in order. Signing happens through the built-in e-signature workflow, which keeps the executed copy attached to the record it belongs to.

The most significant change was obligation tracking. Each supplier agreement is recorded with its key obligations, and renewal alerts are set so that the responsible owner is prompted well ahead of each renewal date. Search across signed contracts allows anyone on the team to find a clause or a commitment without asking a colleague.

## Staff perspective

"We used to rely on one person remembering what was due and when," said Priya Raman, Senior Finance Operations Analyst at Brightwater Finance. "Now the obligations and the renewal dates sit with the contract, and the alerts reach the right owner in time. The team spends its effort deciding what to do about a renewal, not hunting for the document that tells us it is due."

## Measured results

Brightwater Finance now tracks 1,240 supplier obligations in Quillon, each linked to its source agreement and to a named owner. The team has had zero missed renewal dates since go-live. Drafting new agreements from templates and the clause library has also reduced the number of versions circulated for review, and the finance team reports that approvals move through the defined review steps without the follow-up emails that used to slow them down.

Brightwater Finance plans to extend the same setup to additional business units in the coming year, using the templates and approval structure it has already proven.
