# Eastfield & Rowe: customer story

Eastfield & Rowe is a mid-sized law firm with practice groups in commercial litigation, employment, and real estate. Like many firms of its size, it handles a steady flow of engagement letters, non-disclosure agreements, service agreements, and lease documents, each of which must be drafted, reviewed, signed, and then tracked for the duties it creates. This story describes how the firm moved that work onto Quillon and what changed as a result.

## The problem before Quillon

Before adopting Quillon, drafting at Eastfield & Rowe depended on a shared folder of Word templates, many of them several years old. Attorneys copied the closest template, edited it by hand, and then routed the draft by email for review. Approvals were tracked in inboxes, and signed documents were stored in a document management system that few people searched effectively. Obligations such as notice periods, reporting dates, and insurance certificates were recorded in individual spreadsheets, and some of those spreadsheets had not been updated in a long time.

The result was predictable. Engagement letters took too long to produce, clause wording drifted between matters, and the firm had no reliable way to answer questions such as which client agreements contained a particular indemnity cap. A senior partner described the situation this way: the firm was not short of good legal language, but it had no dependable way to find it or reuse it.

## What Eastfield & Rowe set up

The firm adopted Quillon on its Team plan and began with three changes. First, the practice support group rebuilt its most common templates inside Quillon and linked each one to a clause library, so that approved language for liability, confidentiality, termination, and governing law could be inserted rather than retyped. Second, the firm defined a standard approval path for each document type, so that reviews went to the right partner in a set order and every decision was recorded against the document. Third, signing moved to Quillon's e-signature workflow, with signed copies filed automatically alongside the matter.

Obligation tracking came next. Duties extracted from signed agreements, such as reporting deadlines and notice requirements, were entered into Quillon, which keeps them with the contract that created them. Search was configured across all signed contracts, so that a question about a particular clause could be answered in a single query rather than a folder-by-folder review.

## What the firm measured

Eastfield & Rowe tracked three measures during the rollout. The first was the time attorneys spent on drafting and administrative contract work. Across the firm, attorneys now save 11 hours a month on this work, which the firm has redirected toward client matters. The second was the time needed to produce a standard document. A standard engagement letter is now drafted in 6 minutes, down from the earlier process, which depended on manual edits and email review. The third was the consistency of clause language across matters, which the practice support group reports has improved because attorneys now start from the same approved wording.

## Quote from the practice support lead

"We used to rebuild the same engagement letter from memory every time, and we often got the wording slightly wrong. Now the approved language is in the template, the approvals are recorded, and when a client asks what we agreed to three years ago, we can find it in a minute. The biggest change is that people trust the documents they are working from," said Priya Anand, Practice Support Lead at Eastfield & Rowe.

## Looking ahead

The firm plans to extend the same structure to its corporate real estate group in the coming year, and it intends to keep refining its clause library as new matter types emerge. Eastfield & Rowe's experience shows that the largest gains came not from any single feature but from putting templates, approvals, signing, and search in one place, where the work is actually done.
