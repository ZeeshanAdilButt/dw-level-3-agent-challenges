# Ashgrove Legal: customer story

## Who Ashgrove Legal is

Ashgrove Legal is a mid-sized law firm that advises businesses on commercial agreements, employment matters, property transactions and supplier relationships. Its lawyers draft and negotiate a steady flow of contracts for clients ranging from small private companies to established corporate groups. Like many firms of its size, Ashgrove built its working practice around experienced people who knew where the firm's precedents lived and how a good clause was supposed to read. That expertise was a real strength. It also meant that much of the firm's day-to-day work depended on memory, shared drives and personal habits.

This story describes how the firm moved its contract work onto Quillon, a contract-management platform used by law firms and corporate finance teams, and what changed as a result. Ashgrove is on Quillon's Pro plan. The account is described here with the firm's approval, and the firm's staff reviewed the details in this document before publication.

## The problem before Quillon

Before the change, a typical client contract at Ashgrove passed through several hands. A matter lead would ask for a draft, an associate would pull a precedent from a shared folder, and the draft would then be marked up by email. Approvals were requested in the same email threads, which meant that a partner's sign-off could sit unnoticed for days. Once a contract was agreed, the signed copy was often saved under a file name that made sense only to the person who saved it.

The consequences were predictable. Turnaround times stretched, not because the legal work was slow but because the handoffs were. Clients asked for status updates that took time to assemble. Obligations written into signed agreements, such as notice periods, reporting dates and renewal windows, were tracked by individual lawyers and sometimes missed. When a client asked whether a particular type of clause had appeared in previous agreements, answering meant searching several inboxes and asking colleagues who might remember.

The firm's operations lead described the situation plainly: the work was good, but the process around the work was costing the firm time it could have spent advising clients.

## What Ashgrove set up

Ashgrove's rollout began with a decision about scope. Rather than moving every historical document at once, the team chose to start with new client matters and to bring the most-used precedents into Quillon first. The firm built a clause library from its own approved language, organised by agreement type and by the position the firm would take in negotiation. Lawyers could then draft from templates that pulled in the approved clauses, so that a new supplier agreement started from the firm's current standard wording rather than from whichever file someone happened to open.

The firm configured approval routes that matched its internal policy. Routine matters went to a matter lead and then to a supervising partner, while higher-risk agreements added a further review step. Because approvals now happened inside the platform, the person waiting for sign-off received a notification, and the request was visible to everyone involved in the matter. Once terms were agreed, the contract moved to e-signature without leaving the system, and the signed version was filed against the matter automatically.

Obligation tracking was the next piece. For each signed agreement, the firm recorded the key dates and duties that the client needed to remember, including notice periods, reporting deadlines and renewal dates. Quillon's renewal alerts were set to notify the responsible lawyer and the client contact in advance, so that a renewal was no longer a matter of someone remembering to check a calendar. Finally, the firm's search function allowed lawyers to look across all signed contracts for specific clauses or terms, which made it possible to answer a client's question about precedent in minutes rather than days.

Training was kept short and practical. Staff learned the templates and approval routes first, then the search and obligation features, with a named champion in each practice group who could answer questions during the first weeks.

## What staff said

The firm's managing partner, Helen Marsh, summarised the change in her own words:

"We did not set out to change how we practise law. We set out to stop losing time between the stages of a matter. Once the approved language was in one place and the approvals were visible to everyone, our lawyers could spend their attention on the client's problem instead of chasing the paperwork around it."

Helen said the firm's associates were the first to notice the difference, because they were the ones who had previously spent the most time forwarding drafts and searching for precedents. She also noted that the obligation alerts gave the firm's client teams something they had not had before: a reliable way to raise renewals with clients early and in a consistent manner.

## The results

Ashgrove measured its results over the period following the rollout, comparing contract handling against the firm's earlier practice. The most significant change is in how long a contract takes from instruction to completion. Ashgrove Legal cut average contract turnaround from 9 days to 3 days, which the firm attributes mainly to the removal of waiting time between drafting, approval and signature.

The firm also tracked how quickly its internal approvals were completed. Ashgrove Legal had 87% of approvals completed within one business day, a measure the operations team regards as the clearest sign that the approval routes are doing their job. Partners now see requests in one place, and the reminders built into the system mean that approvals are less likely to sit unnoticed.

The firm is careful about what these figures do and do not show. They reflect one firm's practice over one period, and the firm did not attempt to isolate every factor that might have contributed. What the firm is confident about is that the process is now easier to see, easier to repeat and easier to improve.

## Looking ahead

Ashgrove plans to extend the clause library to its remaining practice areas and to bring older signed agreements into the search index over the coming year. The firm's leadership sees the platform less as a finished project than as a shared working space that will keep improving as the firm's precedents are refined.

For other firms considering a similar change, Ashgrove's advice is to begin with the approved language the firm already relies on, to agree the approval routes before configuring them, and to treat obligation tracking as a client service as well as an internal tool. Helen Marsh put it simply: the platform is only as useful as the discipline the firm brings to it, and that discipline is what the firm has built over the course of the rollout.
