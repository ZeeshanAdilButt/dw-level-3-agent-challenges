# Drafting from templates

Quillon's template drafting lets law firms and corporate finance teams produce new contracts from approved starting points, so every agreement begins with language your organization has already reviewed. Instead of copying an old file and hunting for the sections to change, users fill in a structured form and Quillon assembles the document.

## What a template holds

A template is a master document with placeholders, called merge fields, where variable details belong. A single template can hold up to 200 merge fields, which is enough for complex agreements such as multi-party service contracts or financing documents. Each merge field has a label, a data type (text, date, number, or selection from a list), and an optional default value.

## How drafting works

1. Choose a template from the library, or start from a clause set assembled from the clause library.
2. Complete the form. Quillon groups merge fields into logical sections so reviewers can see the inputs at a glance.
3. Review the generated draft. Changes made in the form update the document live, and any clause flagged for approval is routed before the draft can be shared.
4. Send the finished draft for approvals or e-signature.

## Example

A corporate finance team preparing a vendor agreement selects its standard services template. The procurement lead fills in the vendor name, payment terms, and service scope in the form. The legal team, which owns the template, receives a notice only when a clause outside the approved options is selected, so routine agreements move forward without a manual review of every field.

## Keeping templates current

Template owners can update the master version, and Quillon records which drafts were created from each version, so teams can see where a change would apply.
