# Corvane LLP: customer story

Corvane LLP is a commercial law firm with offices across several regions. Its practice covers corporate transactions, employment matters, real estate and disputes, and much of its work arrives in the form of contracts that must be drafted, reviewed, approved, signed and then tracked for years afterward. Like many firms of its size, Corvane found that the volume of paperwork was growing faster than the systems used to manage it. This customer story describes how the firm moved its contract work onto Quillon, the problems it set out to solve, what it configured, and the results its partners and operations team have measured since the rollout.

## The problem before Quillon

Before adopting Quillon, Corvane's contract process was spread across shared drives, email threads and a set of Word templates that had grown independently in each office. Lawyers often started a new agreement by opening an old file, copying the clauses they remembered, and editing from there. The result was inconsistency. Two teams drafting similar agreements could end up with different indemnity wording, different notice periods and different governing-law language, none of which had been deliberately chosen.

Approvals were just as fragmented. A senior associate might send a draft to a partner by email, the partner would reply with comments in a different version, and the final signed copy would sometimes sit in a mailbox rather than in a document store anyone else could search. When a client asked whether a particular agreement contained a termination right or when a renewal fell due, the answer frequently required a manual hunt through several locations and, in some cases, a call to the original matter lead.

Obligation tracking was the most significant gap. Many of the contracts Corvane handles carry ongoing duties: reporting deadlines, payment milestones, insurance certificates and notice requirements. These were recorded in individual calendars or not recorded at all. The firm's risk and operations leaders wanted a single view of obligations across all active agreements, with reminders that did not depend on one person remembering to look.

## What Corvane set up

The firm's operations team led the configuration work with support from practice group leaders. Their first step was to review the Word templates in use across offices and consolidate them into a smaller set of approved master templates. Each master template was then linked to entries in Quillon's clause library, so that standard provisions such as confidentiality, limitation of liability and assignment were drawn from one agreed source rather than retyped.

Drafting from templates in Quillon means that a lawyer starting a new matter selects the appropriate master, answers a short set of questions about the deal, and receives a draft populated with the correct clause entries. Where a negotiation requires departing from the standard position, the lawyer can substitute an alternative clause from the library, and the alternative is recorded so that reviewers can see what changed and why. Approval routing was configured to match the firm's internal sign-off levels, so that drafts move to the right partner in the right order without relying on email chains.

Once agreements were final, they were sent for e-signature through Quillon, and the executed copies were stored in the same repository as the drafts. Corvane also set up obligation tracking for each agreement type, capturing key dates and recurring duties at the point of signature. Renewal alerts were configured to notify matter leads and the central operations team ahead of notice deadlines. Finally, the firm enabled search across all signed contracts, so that a question about a clause or a counterparty could be answered in seconds.

Training was delivered in short sessions tailored to each role. Associates focused on drafting and clause selection, partners on approvals and review, and business support staff on e-signature and search. The firm also appointed a named champion in each office, who handled routine questions and fed back requests for template changes to the operations team.

## The rollout

Corvane chose to roll Quillon out in stages rather than switching every office at once. The first wave covered the largest office, where the volume of new agreements was highest and the benefits of consolidated templates would be most visible. Lessons from that first wave, including a few template wording adjustments and a clearer approval routing structure, were applied before the remaining offices went live.

The full rollout to 11 offices was completed in 5 weeks. Operations staff worked with each office champion to migrate active matters, confirm that obligation dates had been captured correctly, and check that existing signed agreements had been uploaded to the repository in a searchable form. The team kept a running log of issues and resolved most of them within a day. The firm reports that the pace of the rollout was helped by the decision to consolidate templates early, since fewer master documents meant fewer variants for each office to learn.

Adoption has been steady. Associates have adopted the template workflow quickly because it removes the need to start from an old file, and partners have appreciated being able to review changes against a clear baseline rather than comparing multiple versions by hand.

## Measured results

Corvane tracks how new agreements are produced, and the operations team reports that clause reuse has become the normal way of working. Since the rollout, 71% of new contracts are built from clause library entries, which has reduced the amount of retyping and the variation in standard provisions across offices.

The firm also measures how its team uses the platform day to day. Partners describe the approval process as more predictable, and the central team reports that questions about the status of agreements and their upcoming obligations are now answered from the system rather than from individual memory.

## What Corvane's team says

Corvane's head of knowledge and operations, Mara Lindqvist, described the change in practical terms. "We used to be proud of how good our lawyers were at finding the right clause, and that was exactly the problem, because each of them was finding a different one," she said. "Now the good clause is the easy clause. Our partners review the substance of a deal rather than hunting for differences between versions, and when a client asks about a renewal date we can answer it in the time it takes to type the question."

## Looking ahead

Corvane plans to extend its use of obligation tracking to additional agreement types and to refine its clause library as market practice changes. The operations team will review the library on a regular cycle, retiring entries that no longer reflect the firm's positions and adding new ones where negotiations have shown a recurring need. The firm sees the platform as a foundation for consistency rather than a finished project, and expects its templates and approval routing to continue evolving as its practice grows.
