# Search across signed contracts

## What this guide covers

Quillon search lets legal and finance teams find any signed contract in their account by the terms inside it, not just by file name. This guide explains how search works, what it reads, and how to get the most from it. It is written for product users and administrators at law firms and corporate finance teams.

## How the search index stays current

Once a contract is signed, it is added to the search index. The search index updates within 5 minutes of a contract being signed, so a newly executed agreement can be found shortly after the last signature is applied. You do not need to run a manual refresh.

## Scanned documents

Many older agreements exist only as scanned images. Search reads scanned PDFs through OCR up to 300 pages per file, so the text in a scanned agreement becomes searchable in the same way as a native document. Very long scans beyond that page limit should be split into smaller files before upload.

## Example: a finance team locating renewal terms

A finance team preparing for a supplier review needs every agreement with an automatic renewal clause that mentions a notice period. Instead of opening each contract, the team searches for the phrase "automatic renewal" together with "notice," filters to the supplier's name, and sees matching contracts with the surrounding clause text highlighted. The same search also surfaces a scanned agreement from several years earlier that the team had forgotten was in the account.

## Tips for better results

Search the clause language you would expect to see in the contract, not only the title. Use quotation marks for exact phrases, and combine filters such as counterparty or signing date to narrow results. Saved searches can be shared with colleagues who need the same view.
