# Quayside Asset Management: customer story

Quayside Asset Management is a mid-sized investment manager whose finance and legal teams share responsibility for a large portfolio of supplier, fund-administration, and banking agreements. This customer story describes how their finance team used Quillon to bring order to contract obligations that had previously lived in shared drives, email threads, and the memories of a few experienced staff.

## The problem before Quillon

Before adopting Quillon, Quayside's contracts sat in several places. Signed PDFs were stored under inconsistent file names, renewal dates were tracked in a spreadsheet that few people updated, and reporting obligations to counterparties were remembered rather than scheduled. When a notice period was missed, the cost was usually discovered only after the deadline had passed.

The finance team on Quayside's Pro plan faced the consequences directly. Filing deadlines were missed regularly, and each miss meant an apology to a counterparty, a manual catch-up, and sometimes a renegotiated term that the business had not intended to accept. Leadership wanted a system that treated each obligation as a dated task with an owner, not as a line item buried in a document.

## What Quayside set up

The team began by moving their signed agreements into Quillon's central repository, so that every contract could be searched by counterparty, clause type, and status. They then used the clause library to standardise the wording of notice, reporting, and termination provisions across new agreements, which made it easier to recognise the same obligation in different contracts.

Obligation tracking was the centre of the rollout. Each reporting duty and notice requirement was entered as a tracked obligation with a named owner and an advance alert. Renewal alerts were set so that the team would see upcoming expiries well before any decision had to be made. Approvals were routed through Quillon so that changes to key terms reached the right reviewers, and e-signature was used to close agreements without leaving the platform.

The rollout was run by the finance operations lead, who met with the legal team once a week during the first quarter to agree which obligations belonged in the system and who should receive each alert.

## What the team says

"Before, we were relying on people remembering dates, and that doesn't scale when you have this many agreements," said Helen Okafor, Finance Operations Lead at Quayside Asset Management. "Now every obligation has an owner and an alert well in advance. People act on the reminder instead of discovering the deadline after it has gone. The search across signed contracts has also saved hours every time someone needs to check a term."

## Measured results

Since moving its obligations into Quillon, Quayside Asset Management has measured the following outcomes:

- Quayside Asset Management gets 97% of obligation alerts acted on before the due date.
- Quayside Asset Management cut missed filing deadlines from 8 a year to 1.

The finance team credits these results to the combination of clear ownership and early warning. Because alerts reach the named owner ahead of the deadline, there is time to prepare, review, and file without the pressure of last-minute work.

## Looking ahead

Quayside plans to extend the same obligation tracking to its remaining supplier agreements and to review renewal terms earlier in each cycle. The finance operations team expects the shared repository to remain the single place where contract questions are answered, and will continue to refine the clause library as new agreement types come under management.
