# Halden Logistics Finance: customer story

Halden Logistics Finance manages vendor agreements for a freight and warehousing business that works with a large number of carriers, fuel suppliers, equipment lessors, and software providers. Before adopting Quillon, the finance team tracked most of these contracts across shared drives, email attachments, and a spreadsheet that different people updated at different times. This case study describes how the team worked through that problem, what they configured in Quillon, and what changed afterward.

## The problem before Quillon

The finance team's main difficulty was not a lack of contracts but a lack of confidence in them. When a vendor agreement came up for renewal, staff often had to search several folders to find the signed version, then check whether a later amendment had changed the terms. Drafting new agreements meant copying old documents and editing them by hand, which led to inconsistent clauses. Approvals moved through forwarded emails, so it was hard to show who had signed off on what, and in what order. Renewal dates were noted in the spreadsheet, but nobody was reliably alerted before a notice window closed.

The team's lead analyst described the situation this way: "We were spending more time finding the right contract than actually negotiating it. Every renewal felt like a small emergency."

## What the team set up

Halden Logistics Finance began by moving its signed agreements into Quillon and building a standard clause library, so that payment terms, liability limits, and termination language came from approved wording rather than from older documents. The team then created drafting templates for its most common agreement types and connected them to the clause library.

The approval process was the next major piece. The team configured 14 approval templates, each matched to a category of spend and a level of risk, so that routine renewals followed a short path while higher-value or non-standard agreements received additional review. Approvals are recorded inside Quillon, which gives the team a clear trail of each decision.

Signing happens through the built-in e-signature step, and once a contract is executed it is stored with its obligations logged. The team recorded key commitments, such as delivery service levels and reporting deadlines, so they appear as tracked items. Renewal alerts were set to notify the responsible person ahead of each notice window, and full-text search across signed contracts allowed staff to locate a clause or a counterparty in seconds.

## Measured results

After the rollout, Halden Logistics Finance cut vendor contract cycle time by 38%, measured from the point a contract request was opened to the point it was fully executed. The team attributes most of this gain to template-based drafting and the shorter approval path for routine work, since staff no longer had to rebuild documents or chase signatures through email.

The analyst reflected on the change: "The biggest difference is that I trust the record now. When someone asks what we agreed to with a carrier, I can answer from the contract itself, and I know a renewal is coming before it becomes urgent."

Halden Logistics Finance continues to refine its templates and approval rules as its vendor base changes, and it reports that the structured setup has made onboarding new finance staff considerably simpler.
