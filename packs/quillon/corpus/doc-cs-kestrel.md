# Kestrel Wealth: customer story

Kestrel Wealth is a wealth management firm that works with private clients and family offices. Its finance team handles a steady flow of client agreements, engagement letters, and supplier contracts, and until recently those documents moved through a patchwork of shared drives, email threads, and printed signature pages. This case study describes how the team moved that work onto Quillon, what they set up, and what changed.

## The problem before Quillon

Before adopting Quillon, the finance team tracked contracts by hand. Drafts were copied from old files, so clause wording drifted from one agreement to the next. Approvals happened over email, which made it hard to show who had signed off on what and when. Once a contract was signed, the obligations inside it, such as reporting deadlines and fee review dates, lived in individual people's calendars. Renewals were frequently noticed only after the window had closed.

Signing itself was slow. A typical agreement took several days to reach full execution, because documents were printed, couriered, scanned, and filed again. Staff spent much of their time chasing signatures rather than managing the relationships the contracts described.

## What Kestrel Wealth set up

The team began by building a shared clause library in Quillon, drawing on the approved wording from their most-used agreements. New engagement letters are now drafted from templates that pull in those clauses, so standard language stays consistent while deal-specific terms are added where needed.

Approvals are routed inside the platform. Each draft moves through a defined sequence of reviewers, and every decision is recorded against the document. E-signature replaces the printing and courier step, and signed copies are stored automatically alongside their approval history.

The team also switched on obligation tracking and renewal alerts. Key dates from each signed contract are captured as trackable items, and the responsible person receives a reminder ahead of each deadline. Search across all signed contracts means a question such as which agreements include a particular reporting term can be answered in seconds rather than by opening folders one at a time.

The finance team uses the Pro plan, which covers the full set of drafting, approval, signing, and tracking features described above.

## Results

Over its first year on Quillon, Kestrel Wealth signed 2,300 contracts through the platform. Average signing time fell from 3 days to 4 hours, which means agreements that once waited most of a week for full execution now typically close within a single working session.

Beyond speed, the team reports that renewals are no longer missed, that approvals are easy to audit, and that clause wording is consistent across the firm's agreements.
