# Arriving late

## Purpose of this policy

This policy explains how Juniper Lane Clinic in Port Avery plans its appointment day, what patients can expect when they arrive for a booked visit, and how we handle arrivals that are early or late. It applies to every service we offer, including family medicine, child health checks, vaccinations and our travel clinic, blood tests, physiotherapy, women's health appointments, minor procedures, repeat prescription administration and telehealth calls. Staff at every level are expected to follow it, and patients are encouraged to read it so they know what to expect when they book.

## How the clinic day is scheduled

Our clinicians work in sessions, and each session is built from appointments of a fixed length. A family medicine appointment is planned with enough time for a full history, examination and discussion of options. Child health checks follow a set pattern because they include growth measurements, developmental questions and, where relevant, immunization review. Vaccination slots are shorter but are timed around the nursing team's preparation and the observation period that follows each dose. Travel clinic consultations take longer than standard vaccination visits because they involve reviewing destinations, planning doses over several weeks and discussing preventive advice.

Blood test appointments are arranged so that the phlebotomy room is used efficiently, with samples labelled and dispatched on a regular schedule. Physiotherapy sessions are booked back to back in treatment rooms, and the therapist's notes from one session often shape the plan for the next, so the timing of each slot matters for continuity. Women's health appointments and minor procedures require sterile preparation, consent discussion and, for some procedures, a post-procedure recovery window before the next patient can be taken into the same room. Repeat prescription administration and telehealth calls are shorter but are still scheduled as discrete blocks so that clinicians can review records and prepare before speaking with a patient.

Because each clinician's list is planned this way, a delay at the start of one appointment affects the appointments that follow it. A consultation that begins late, or a room that is not ready, can push back several later bookings. We plan some flexibility into each session, but the flexibility is limited, and it is the reason our arrival rules exist. When patients arrive on time, the clinic runs close to its published times, waiting rooms stay manageable, and staff are able to give each patient the attention their appointment was booked for.

We also recognise that patients have their own constraints, including work, school pick-ups, transport and caring responsibilities. Our reception team will always try to help patients who have a genuine reason for a late or early arrival, and they can often suggest a change of time if a booked slot is no longer workable. The guidance below is designed to protect everyone's appointment, not to penalise patients who have difficulty getting to the clinic.

## Arriving before your appointment

Patients who arrive early are asked to wait in the reception area and check in at the desk when they are ready, rather than going straight to a consultation room. This allows our team to confirm details, update records and make sure the correct room is prepared. Patients arriving 5 minutes early are welcome to check in and wait for their appointment time, and new patients arrive 10 minutes early so that registration forms, identity checks and any required paperwork can be completed before the appointment starts. Staff will not call a patient into a room before the room has been cleaned and prepared, so early arrival does not normally bring the appointment forward.

Some services have a stricter requirement. A glucose tolerance test cannot start late, so patients arrive 10 minutes early for that test. The preparation includes checking the patient's current state, explaining the sequence of samples and confirming that the first sample can be taken at the correct time. If the test cannot begin on schedule, the timing of every later sample is affected, so this is one area where early arrival is essential rather than simply helpful.

## Arriving late

Lateness affects us all, and we aim to be fair to patients who run behind. A patient arriving up to 10 minutes late for a general appointment is still seen, though the appointment will be shorter to make up the time, and the clinician may need to focus on the most important issues. If a patient arrives more than 10 minutes late, the reception team will check whether the clinician can still see them without disrupting the next patients, and in many cases the appointment will be rebooked. Patients who arrive late should always let reception know, because a call or message can help the team decide quickly what is possible.

Physiotherapy has a shorter grace period, because each session is tied closely to the treatment plan and to the next booking in the same treatment room. For physiotherapy the late limit is 5 minutes, and a patient arriving later than that may be offered a shortened session, a rebooked appointment or, where appropriate, an alternative time later the same day. Patients attending physiotherapy are therefore encouraged to plan their journey with some margin.

Telehealth calls work differently because there is no waiting room to manage. If a patient is late joining a call, the clinician will try to connect within the booked block, but the call may be shortened or moved if the next scheduled call has already begun. Patients should join using the link provided a few minutes ahead of time and check their connection before the call.

## Missed and cancelled appointments

If a patient cannot attend, we ask that they tell us as soon as possible so that the slot can be offered to someone else. Cancelling early helps patients who are waiting for an appointment and allows our clinicians to use their time well. Where a patient repeatedly arrives late or does not attend without notice, our reception team may discuss the pattern with the patient and suggest ways to make attendance easier, such as adjusting appointment times or offering telehealth where clinically suitable.

## Exceptions and concerns

Some situations are outside a patient's control, including transport failure, emergencies affecting a family member, sudden illness on the way to the clinic and severe weather. Patients who are delayed for these reasons should contact us as soon as they can. Our team will take each case on its merits, and a patient who explains the situation promptly will usually be offered the most reasonable option available on the day.

If a patient feels that this policy has been applied unfairly, they may raise the matter with the practice manager at the front desk or in writing. Concerns will be reviewed, and the outcome will be explained to the patient. Clinical care is never withheld because of lateness alone where a clinician judges that the patient needs to be seen, and staff are expected to use their judgement when a delay could affect a patient's health.

## Review of this policy

This policy takes effect on 2026-03-01 and replaces any earlier guidance on arrival times at Juniper Lane Clinic. We review it regularly, and changes will be published at reception and on our website. Questions about any part of it can be directed to our reception team during opening hours.
