# Hand-off queue guide

## Purpose

This guide explains how work is passed between reception, clinical staff and the telehealth desk at Juniper Lane Clinic in Port Avery. Every hand-off placed in the queue must be triaged, routed and closed in a consistent way so that patients receive the right level of attention without delay. It applies to all staff who create, receive or respond to hand-offs, including those covering family medicine, child health checks, vaccinations and the travel clinic, blood tests, physiotherapy, women's health, minor procedures, repeat prescription administration and telehealth calls.

## Ownership

The hand-off queue is owned by reception lead Faye Dunmore. She sets the daily priorities, reviews the queue at the start and end of each session, and decides when a backlog requires additional cover. Questions about how the queue is run, changes to categories, or disputes over priority should be referred to her in the first instance. Clinical decisions remain with the treating clinician and are not overridden by queue ownership.

## Priority levels

Each hand-off must be given one of three priority levels when it is created. The level determines the response standard that applies.

Urgent hand-offs cover any situation where a patient may be at immediate risk, where a clinician has asked for same-session contact, or where a patient reports symptoms that the triage protocol flags as needing prompt review. An urgent hand-off must be picked up within 5 minutes of being placed in the queue. Urgent hand-offs are answered in 5 minutes, soon in 2 hours and routine by the next working day, meaning the response standard depends on the priority assigned. Staff must not downgrade a hand-off to avoid an urgent response.

Soon hand-offs cover matters that need a response within the same working day but are not an immediate risk, such as a results query that a patient is waiting on or a prescription request with a short supply remaining. These hand-offs are answered within 2 hours during opening times.

Routine hand-offs cover administrative tasks, non-urgent correspondence, and requests that do not affect current care. These are answered by the next working day.

## Creating a hand-off

When creating a hand-off, record the patient identifier, the reason for contact, the requested action, the assigned priority and the name of the staff member who created it. Keep the description factual and brief. Do not include information in the free-text field that is not needed for the task. Attach any relevant results or prescription details to the record rather than copying them into the message.

## Picking up and closing

The first person to open an urgent or soon hand-off should mark it as picked up so that colleagues do not duplicate effort. Once the action is complete, record the outcome and close the hand-off. If the action cannot be completed, reassign the hand-off with a note explaining why, and do not leave it open without an owner.

## Escalation

If an urgent hand-off has not been picked up within 5 minutes, the creator should notify the reception lead directly. If a clinical concern is involved, the on-duty clinician must be informed at the same time. Repeated breaches of response standards should be reported to Faye Dunmore so that the queue can be reviewed.

## Review

This guide takes effect on 2026-09-01. Faye Dunmore will review it when the queue structure changes or when response times are consistently missed, and staff will be notified of any revision.
