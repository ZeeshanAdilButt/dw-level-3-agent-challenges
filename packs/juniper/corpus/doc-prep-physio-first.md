# Preparing for your first physiotherapy visit

This guide explains what to expect and how to prepare for your first physiotherapy assessment at Juniper Lane Clinic in Port Avery. Our physiotherapy service works with patients of all ages who have pain, stiffness, reduced movement, sports injuries, or recovery needs after illness or surgery. Taking a little time beforehand helps the physiotherapist understand your problem and plan your care from the first session.

## Before your appointment

Please bring any scan reports you have, such as X-rays, MRI results, or ultrasound reports, so the physiotherapist can review them with you. If you do not have copies, tell us where the scans were done and we will request them where possible. Scan reports are helpful but not essential, and a missing report will not stop your assessment from going ahead.

It also helps to bring a written list of activities that cause trouble. Think about movements and tasks such as walking, climbing stairs, lifting, driving, working at a desk, sleeping, or playing a particular sport. For each one, note roughly how it affects you and how long the symptoms last afterwards. This list gives the physiotherapist a clear picture of what matters most to you in daily life.

Wear comfortable clothing that allows easy movement of the area being assessed. Shorts or loose trousers are useful for leg or hip problems, and a vest or fitted top is helpful for shoulder, neck, or back problems. Bring any footwear or supports you normally use, such as insoles, braces, or walking aids.

Please also make a note of any medications you take, any previous injuries or operations, and any conditions that might affect exercise, such as heart or breathing problems. If you have recently had a fall or a new symptom, mention it when you arrive so the team can assess whether anything needs urgent review.

## What happens at the assessment

The first visit usually begins with a conversation about your history, your goals, and the problem you want help with. The physiotherapist will ask when the symptoms started, what made them begin, what eases them, and what makes them worse. This discussion is the foundation of the assessment, so please be as open and specific as you can.

Next, the physiotherapist will examine the relevant part of your body. This may include looking at posture, measuring the range of movement in joints, testing strength, and checking how you move during simple tasks such as standing up, bending, or walking. You will be asked to move gently and to tell the physiotherapist if anything is uncomfortable. You are always in control of the examination, and you can ask for a pause or a change at any time.

At the end of the assessment, the physiotherapist will explain what they found, discuss a likely diagnosis where possible, and agree a plan with you. The plan may include exercises to do at home, advice on activity levels, hands-on treatment in clinic, and a realistic timeline for improvement. You will leave with written notes so you can refer back to them.

## After your visit

Follow-up appointments are arranged according to your plan, and you can book them through reception or by telephone. If your symptoms change significantly between visits, such as new numbness, weakness, or severe pain, please contact the clinic promptly. For emergencies, contact emergency services without delay.

If you have questions before your first visit, please speak with our reception team, who can help you prepare and answer general queries about the service.
