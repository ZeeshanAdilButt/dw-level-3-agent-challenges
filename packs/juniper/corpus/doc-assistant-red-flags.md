# When to call 112

## Purpose and scope

This policy explains which symptoms need an emergency call, how the Juniper Lane Clinic virtual assistant responds when a patient describes a warning sign, how nurse call-backs work, and what front desk staff can and cannot say about medicines. It applies to all patients using Juniper Lane Clinic services in Port Avery, including family medicine, child health checks, vaccinations and the travel clinic, blood tests, physiotherapy, women's health, minor procedures, repeat prescription administration and telehealth calls.

This policy takes effect on 2026-09-01.

## In an emergency

1. In an emergency call 112 now. Do not wait for a clinic appointment, a call-back or a reply through the online assistant.
2. Do not drive yourself or someone else to hospital if the person is unwell, confused or collapsing. Call 112 so that trained responders can advise and attend.
3. If you are with someone who has stopped breathing or is unresponsive, call 112 immediately and follow the instructions the call handler gives you.

## Red flag symptoms that mean call 112 now

The following are reasons to call 112 now. You do not need to check with the clinic first.

1. Chest pain with numbness in the arm is a reason to call 112 now. Do not wait to see whether it passes.
2. Sudden face drooping, arm weakness or slurred speech are stroke signs and a reason to call 112 now. Note the time the symptoms started and tell the call handler.
3. Severe trouble breathing is a reason to call 112 now, including breathlessness at rest, inability to speak in full sentences or blue lips.
4. A severe allergic reaction with a swelling face or throat is a reason to call 112 now. This includes possible anaphylaxis, where breathing or swallowing becomes difficult after a sting, food or medicine.
5. Thoughts of suicide are a reason to call 112 now. If you are not in immediate danger but feel unsafe with those thoughts, tell a trusted person and contact your clinician urgently.
6. Heavy bleeding that will not stop is a reason to call 112 now. Apply firm pressure to the wound while you wait for help.

If you are unsure whether a symptom is serious, treat it as urgent and call 112. It is always acceptable to call 112 and be advised that you do not need an ambulance.

## How the virtual assistant responds

The Juniper Lane Clinic virtual assistant is a support tool. It can help with booking, directions, opening times and general information. It is not a clinician and it does not diagnose.

1. When a patient describes a red flag, the assistant tells them to call 112 and alerts staff at once.
2. The assistant does not ask the patient to complete a questionnaire or wait for a reply before giving the 112 instruction.
3. After giving the instruction, the assistant alerts staff so that the clinic team can follow up with the patient where appropriate.
4. The assistant does not give advice on medicines, doses or weight loss, and it will not try to reassure a patient that a red flag symptom is probably minor.

## Urgent but non-emergency problems

For urgent problems that are not emergencies, such as a high fever in a young child that is not causing the red flags above, or a sudden worsening of a known condition where you are concerned but are not in immediate danger, use the out-of-hours line on 0155 555 0199. Outside clinic hours, this line is the route for urgent but non-emergency problems.

If the problem becomes severe at any point, stop and call 112.

## Nurse call-backs for non-urgent questions

1. For a non-urgent clinical question, request a nurse call-back through the clinic.
2. A nurse call-back for a non-urgent clinical question is arranged within 1 working day.
3. When you request a call-back, give a brief description of the question and a phone number where you can be reached.
4. Do not use a call-back request for symptoms that may be red flags. Those need 112 first.

## Medicines questions

1. Front desk staff do not give advice about medicines, doses or changes. Reception does not give advice about medicines, doses or changes, and sends those questions to a clinician.
2. A patient unsure about their medicines phones the nurse line, because the assistant cannot advise on medicines. Do not stop, start or change a medicine based on what you read online or what the assistant says.
3. If you are unsure about a medicine and feel unwell after taking it, call 112 if the reaction is a red flag above. Otherwise, phone the nurse line.
4. Repeat prescription requests are handled through the usual clinic process, and staff can confirm the status of a request without advising on the medicine itself.

## What front desk staff can and cannot say

Front desk staff can:

1. Confirm opening times, booking options and how to reach the nurse line or out-of-hours line.
2. Tell a caller that a red flag symptom means call 112 now.
3. Take a message and arrange a nurse call-back for a non-urgent clinical question.

Front desk staff cannot:

1. Advise on medicines, doses or changes to treatment.
2. Advise on weight loss or any dosing decision.
3. Tell a caller that a red flag symptom is probably not serious.

## Responsibilities

Clinical leads are responsible for keeping this policy current and for reviewing it whenever the emergency or out-of-hours arrangements change. All staff who answer phones or use the virtual assistant must read this policy before their first shift and whenever it is updated. Anyone who is unsure how to respond to a caller should treat the situation as urgent and follow the call 112 rule.

## Review

This policy will be reviewed regularly by the clinic leadership team. Patients who have questions about it can ask at reception or use the nurse line during opening hours.
