# Acting for someone else

**Policy type:** Rule
**Audience:** Public
**Effective from:** 1 February 2026
**Applies to:** All patients, parents, carers and representatives who contact Juniper Lane Clinic in Port Avery

## Purpose

This policy explains who may book appointments, ask questions, collect information or make decisions on behalf of another patient at Juniper Lane Clinic. It sets out what proof we need before we act for someone else, and it explains the limits that apply even when someone has authority. The aim is to protect each patient's privacy while making it straightforward for families and carers to get the care they need.

## Who this policy covers

This policy applies to every request made by one person on behalf of another, whether the request is made in person, by telephone, through our online portal, by email or during a telehealth call. It covers family medicine, child health checks, vaccinations, the travel clinic, blood tests, physiotherapy, women's health services, minor procedures, repeat prescription administration and telehealth consultations.

## Adults acting for adults

An adult patient is entitled to make their own decisions about their health care and to decide who else is told about it. If you wish to act for another adult, such as a spouse, partner, parent, adult child or friend, the patient must give you authority in writing before we will discuss their care with you or make bookings on their behalf.

That written authority is Form CF-21. The form is the signed authority that lets one adult act for another adult. It must be completed and signed by the patient, and it must name the person being authorised and the kinds of tasks they may carry out. We keep a copy on the patient's record. Authority remains in place until the patient withdraws it in writing or until the clinic is notified that it no longer applies.

Without Form CF-21 on file, our staff can take a message, pass on general clinic information such as opening times, and tell you how the patient can give us authority. We cannot confirm test results, discuss a diagnosis or change a treatment plan for someone who has not given us permission to do so.

## Parents and carers of children

Parents and legal guardians normally have the right to make decisions for a child and to receive information about that child's care. Where a child is young, our clinicians will usually speak with the parent or guardian at the child's appointments. As children grow older, they are increasingly involved in decisions about their own care, and clinicians may speak with the young person privately for part of a consultation. This is standard practice and is intended to support the young person's confidence in seeking care.

Where a person other than a parent or guardian brings a child in, such as a grandparent, relative or childminder, we will ask for evidence that they have the parent or guardian's agreement to act. Our staff will explain what we need at the time of booking.

## Linked family members and the patient portal

Our online patient portal allows one account holder to manage bookings for family members who are linked to that account. A patient can book for linked family members once authority is on file. Authority is confirmed through Form CF-21 for adults, and through the appropriate parental or guardian confirmation for children.

Once a family member is linked, the account holder can see upcoming appointments and make new bookings for that person. The portal does not give access to a linked person's clinical notes, test results or messages unless separate written consent is also in place. If you are unsure whether a family member is linked, contact the front desk before attempting to book.

## Test results and sharing information with others

Results of blood tests and other investigations belong to the patient. We will not send results to a third party, including a family member, employer, insurer, school or another organisation, unless the patient has given written consent for that specific release. Consent must name who may receive the information and what information may be shared. A general statement that someone may be told everything is not enough for us to rely on.

Where a patient is unable to give consent because they are unwell or lack capacity, our clinicians will decide what information may be shared in the patient's best interests and will record the reasons for that decision. Clinicians may also share information where the law requires it or where there is a serious risk of harm.

## Representatives and power of attorney

Some people hold a legal document that gives them authority to make decisions for another person. If you hold a lasting power of attorney, a court-appointed deputy or another formal legal authority, please show us the original or a certified copy before we act on it. We will check that the document covers the decisions you wish to make, such as health and welfare decisions, and we will keep a copy on file. Our staff may ask questions to confirm that the document is still valid.

## Requests we cannot accept

We will not act for another person where we have not seen the required authority, where we have reason to believe the patient has not agreed to the request, or where the request would put the patient or someone else at risk. If a request is refused, our staff will explain why and tell you what you can do next, including how to obtain the right form or document.

## Responsibilities

Patients are responsible for keeping their authority up to date and for telling us promptly if they wish to withdraw it. Anyone acting for another person is responsible for using that authority only for the purposes agreed and for keeping the information they receive confidential. Clinic staff are responsible for checking authority before acting, recording it accurately and explaining these requirements clearly.

## Questions and concerns

If you are unsure whether you can act for someone, please ask the front desk before you make a booking or request information. You can also speak with a member of the clinical team during a telehealth call or at your next appointment. If you believe information has been shared without proper authority, please tell us as soon as possible so that we can investigate and respond.

## Review

This policy takes effect on 1 February 2026 and will be reviewed regularly to make sure it remains accurate and easy to follow. Updated versions will be published on the clinic's website and made available at reception.
