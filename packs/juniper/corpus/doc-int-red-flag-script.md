# Front desk red flag script

## Purpose and authority

This playbook sets the required response when a caller at Juniper Lane Clinic describes symptoms that may signal a medical emergency. It is a rule for all front desk staff, effective from 2026-09-01. Staff must follow these steps exactly, in order, and must not improvise a different route for a red flag call.

## What counts as a red flag

A red flag is any description from a caller that suggests a risk to life or serious harm now. Examples include (but are not limited to) difficulty breathing, chest pain or pressure, sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body, confusion or fitting, heavy bleeding, loss of consciousness, severe allergic reaction, or a child who is struggling to breathe or is unresponsive. When in doubt, treat the call as a red flag.

## Numbered steps

1. Listen for the key words in the caller's first description and decide quickly whether it is a red flag.
2. Interrupt politely if needed and say clearly: put the phone down and call 112 now.
3. Ask the caller to stay on the line only if they cannot call 112 themselves, and tell them you will not leave them.
4. Do not place the caller on hold at any point. A caller describing a red flag is never put on hold for more than 30 seconds, so if you need to check anything, speak to them directly.
5. Do not name a possible condition. Staff never name a possible condition on a red flag call, even if you think you know what it is. Describe only what the caller must do next.
6. If the caller is with a patient who is not responsive, ask whether they are breathing normally and instruct them to call 112 immediately.
7. Keep the call brief. Confirm the caller's name, callback number and the address where the patient is, if the caller gives them freely.
8. Once the call ends, notify the clinician on duty without delay so they are aware of the situation.
9. Record the call in the incident register. Every red flag call is logged in the incident register within 1 hour of the call ending, with the time, the words used, the action you took and who you informed.
10. Do not offer an appointment, a telehealth slot or a repeat prescription in place of emergency care for a red flag call.

## Escalation and follow up

The clinician on duty reviews each entry in the incident register on the same working day. If you are unsure whether a call was a red flag, log it anyway and mark it for review. Do not discuss individual calls with people outside the clinic except as directed by the clinical lead.

## Training and sign off

All front desk staff must read this playbook and confirm in writing that they have done so before working a telephone shift. The clinical lead reviews this script at least once a year and whenever the call handling process changes.
