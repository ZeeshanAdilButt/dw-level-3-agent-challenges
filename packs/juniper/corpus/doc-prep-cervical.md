# Preparing for cervical screening

## About this guide

This guide is for patients booking a cervical screening appointment at Juniper Lane Clinic in Port Avery. It explains what the test involves, how to prepare, and what happens afterwards. If you have questions that are not answered here, please speak with a member of our reception team or ask your clinician at your appointment.

## Why cervical screening matters

Cervical screening checks the cells of the cervix (the lower part of the womb) for changes that could develop into cancer if left untreated. Screening does not test for every health concern, but it is one of the most effective ways to find changes early, when they are easiest to treat. Many people feel anxious before a screening appointment, and that is normal. Our clinicians are used to talking patients through the process at a comfortable pace.

## Before your appointment

There are a few simple steps that can help the test go smoothly. Where possible, try to avoid using vaginal creams, lubricants, pessaries or other vaginal products for at least two days before your appointment, as these can affect the sample. If you are on your period on the day of your appointment, you can still attend, but please let the clinician know so they can advise whether it is better to rebook. Many people find it helpful to empty their bladder shortly before the appointment and to wear clothing that is easy to remove from the waist down.

If you have had a previous abnormal result, a procedure on your cervix, or you are unsure about your screening history, please mention this when you book so the appointment can be planned properly.

## During your appointment

Your clinician will explain each step before it happens and will check that you are comfortable throughout. You will lie on an examination couch with a sheet covering you. A speculum (a smooth, medical instrument) is gently placed into the vagina so the cervix can be seen, and a small brush or spatula is used to collect cells from the cervix. Some people feel pressure or mild discomfort, while others feel very little. If at any point you would like the clinician to stop or pause, you can say so and they will do so.

You are welcome to bring a chaperone with you, and you can ask for a chaperone to be present if you would prefer one. Please let reception know when you book if you have specific needs, such as a preference for a clinician of a particular gender or a need for extra time.

## After your appointment

You may have some light spotting for a short time afterwards, which is common. Contact us if you have heavy bleeding, a fever, or pain that is getting worse.

Your sample is sent to a laboratory for analysis. Cervical screening results arrive by letter within 14 days, so you do not need to call us to ask for them. If your result shows that further checks are needed, we will contact you to arrange the next steps and explain what they involve.

## Rebooking

If you would rather rebook your appointment on the day, for example because you are unwell or your period has started, you can do so with no fee. Please let reception know as early as possible so that another patient can be offered the slot.

## Contact us

If you have questions about cervical screening, please contact our reception team during opening hours or book a telehealth call with a clinician if you would prefer to talk things through first.
