# Preparing for the travel clinic

## About this guide

Juniper Lane Clinic is a family practice serving the people of Port Avery and the surrounding area. Our services include family medicine, child health checks, vaccinations and a travel clinic, blood tests, physiotherapy, women's health, minor procedures, repeat prescription administration and telehealth calls. This guide is written for patients who are planning an overseas trip and want to understand how our travel clinic works, what to prepare, and how to make the most of the consultation. It applies to all patients of the practice, including children travelling with a parent or guardian, and it is intended to be read alongside any advice your clinician gives you during your appointment.

Travel health needs vary a great deal. A short business trip to a city with reliable healthcare has very different requirements from a months-long trek through remote areas, and the destination, the season, the length of stay, the type of activity planned and your own medical history all shape what is recommended. For this reason, the travel clinic does not work from a single standard checklist. Instead, each consultation is built around your individual plans. This guide explains the general process so that you can arrive prepared and get the most out of your time with the clinician.

## Why planning early matters

Many travel health measures take time to work. Some vaccines need several weeks to build protection, some courses of medicine must be started before you leave, and certain vaccines are given as a series of doses spaced apart. If you leave it until the last minute, you may find that some recommended protections cannot be completed before your departure. Early planning gives the clinician room to offer the full range of options, and it gives you time to arrange any supplies, insurance or documents you may need.

It also helps to think ahead about the practical side of travel health. Consider whether you have enough time to attend follow-up appointments, whether your employer or school needs any health paperwork, and whether any family members travelling with you have their own requirements. Children often need a different schedule from adults, and some pregnant travellers or people with long-term conditions need extra consideration. Raising these points early means they can be addressed properly rather than rushed.

## Planning your trip

Before you contact the practice, spend some time mapping out the details of your journey. A clear picture of where you are going and what you will be doing makes the consultation far more productive. Start by listing every country you plan to visit, including any transit stops where you might leave the airport or spend a night. Note the order in which you will visit them and roughly how long you will stay in each place.

Next, think about what you will actually be doing. Are you staying in hotels in large cities, or in rural guesthouses? Will you be eating local food, swimming in lakes or rivers, hiking, camping, working in a clinic or volunteering? Will you be spending time around animals, or in places where insects are common? Each of these activities carries different health considerations, and the clinician will want to understand them in order to tailor advice. It is also worth noting the time of year you are travelling, since seasonal weather, rainfall and insect activity can all change the risks in a particular location.

Consider your own health as well. Think about any ongoing conditions, past surgeries, allergies, current medicines and any previous reactions to vaccines or travel medicines. If you are pregnant or planning a pregnancy, say so when you book, as this affects which vaccines and medicines are suitable. If you have a condition that is well controlled but requires regular monitoring, think about how you will keep up with that care while away. Carrying a summary of your medical history can be helpful, particularly if you are travelling to a place where you may need to see a local doctor.

Finally, think about the logistics of the trip itself. Check the validity of your passport, since many countries require it to remain valid for several months beyond your planned return date. Consider whether you will need visas, and whether your travel insurance covers the activities you have planned. If you are travelling with children, make sure you have the correct consent documents for each country. None of these tasks is strictly a medical matter, but a clinician can only give you comprehensive advice if they understand the full picture of your journey.

Once you have a clear outline of your plans, write it down in a form you can easily bring to your appointment. A simple list or table will do. The more specific you can be about dates and destinations, the more accurately the clinician can plan your care.

## Booking your travel clinic consultation

Travel clinic appointments are booked through the front desk or the online booking system. Please book the travel clinic consultation at least 6 weeks before departure, as this gives time for vaccines that need multiple doses or a build-up period to be completed, and it leaves room for any follow-up appointments that may be recommended. If your departure is sooner than six weeks away, please still call the practice. The clinician can advise on what can be done in the time available, and some measures can still be useful even with a shorter lead time.

When you book, please tell the reception team the destination countries, your approximate travel dates and whether you are travelling with anyone else who will need an appointment. Let them know if you are pregnant, have a known allergy, or have a condition that may affect your choices. This information helps the practice allocate an appointment of the right length and with the right clinician. Appointments for children and for patients with complex needs may require more time, so booking the correct type of slot matters.

If you need to change or cancel your appointment, please let us know as early as you can, so that the slot can be offered to another patient. Where a consultation has been arranged in advance, the reception team will be able to confirm the details and answer any questions about preparation.

## Preparing for your consultation

To make your consultation as efficient as possible, please bring a small set of documents and information with you. The most important of these is your travel itinerary with dates and countries, so bring the travel itinerary with dates and countries to the travel clinic consultation. A printed copy or a clear screenshot on your phone is perfectly acceptable. The clinician will use this to work out which vaccines, medicines and precautions apply to each stop on your route, and to identify the places where risks change.

Also bring your vaccination record to the travel clinic consultation. This is the document that shows which vaccines you have already received and when. Many countries and many vaccines are recorded in a personal health card, a booklet or an electronic record. Having this to hand allows the clinician to see what protection you already have, which vaccines are still due, and whether any boosters are needed. If you cannot find your record, tell the clinician, as the practice can often check previous immunisations from your notes, though it is helpful to have your own copy as well.

It is also useful to bring a list of any medicines you currently take, including over-the-counter products and supplements, along with any allergies you know of. If you have travel insurance, bring the policy details or a note of the insurer's contact number, as this may be relevant to your planning. Finally, bring any questions you have written down in advance. A consultation can cover a lot of ground, and having your questions ready ensures nothing important is forgotten.

## What happens at the consultation

The consultation begins with a discussion of your travel plans and medical history. The clinician will go through your itinerary with you, country by country, and will ask about your planned activities and accommodation. This conversation helps establish which health risks are relevant to you specifically. The clinician will then review your vaccination record and advise on which vaccines are recommended, which are optional, and which may be required for entry to certain countries.

Where vaccines are recommended, the clinician will explain the benefits, the possible side effects and the timing of each dose. Some vaccines can be given on the same day, while others need to be spaced apart. Your clinician will help you understand the schedule so that you know exactly what to expect. If a vaccine is not suitable for you because of an allergy, a medical condition or pregnancy, the clinician will explain the alternatives.

The consultation may also cover preventive measures that do not involve vaccines or medicines. These can include advice on food and water safety, protection against insect bites, sun protection, personal hygiene and the signs of illness that should prompt you to seek care while abroad. Where medicines such as antimalarial tablets, antibiotics for travellers' diarrhoea or motion sickness treatments are appropriate, the clinician will explain how to take them and what to watch for.

Please note that the travel clinic gives advice and administers the recommended measures, but it cannot guarantee that you will avoid illness, and some risks cannot be removed entirely. The clinician will be honest about the level of protection that different measures provide, so that you can make an informed decision.

## After your consultation

Following your appointment, you may receive a printed summary of the advice given, together with details of any vaccines administered and any follow-up that has been arranged. Keep this summary with your travel documents and your vaccination record. If you were given a prescription or a course of medicine, please make sure you understand when and how to take it, and check that you have enough supply for your trip.

If you were advised to return for further doses or a follow-up review, please book that appointment promptly so that it fits into your timetable. If anything about your plans changes after the consultation, such as a new destination or an extended stay, please contact the practice so that the advice can be updated. You can usually arrange a short telehealth call for simple questions, although some vaccines and reviews need to be seen in person.

Finally, remember that travel health advice is most useful when it is acted on well before you leave and kept with you while you are away. If you become unwell during your trip, seek medical care promptly, and when you return, let the practice know about any illness or treatment you received abroad. This helps us keep your records complete and supports your ongoing care.

## Contact and further help

For questions about this guide or to book a travel clinic consultation, please contact the reception team at Juniper Lane Clinic during opening hours. For urgent health concerns, please seek care from the nearest emergency service rather than waiting for a routine appointment. We look forward to helping you prepare for a safe and healthy journey.
