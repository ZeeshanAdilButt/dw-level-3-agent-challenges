# Larkfield Friendly Society

## About this page

This page is written for members of Larkfield Friendly Society who receive care at Juniper Lane Clinic in Port Avery. It explains how the society works alongside the clinic's services, what members can expect when they attend for care, and how to make sure that costs are handled correctly. The clinic is a family practice, and its services include family medicine, child health checks, vaccinations and a travel clinic, blood tests, physiotherapy, women's health, minor procedures, repeat prescription administration and telehealth calls. Each of these areas may be affected by the society's rules in a different way, so members are encouraged to read the sections that apply to the care they are planning.

Membership with Larkfield Friendly Society is designed to help families plan for routine and planned healthcare. Many members use the clinic for ongoing care, such as regular check-ups for children, repeat prescriptions for long-term conditions, or follow-up after a procedure. Others use it for occasional needs, such as a vaccination before travel or a blood test requested by their doctor. The society's rules are the same whichever type of care a member uses, but the way they apply can depend on the service, so this page sets out the general principles first and then describes the more specific arrangements.

Members should keep this page for reference. The rules described here take effect on 1 April 2026 and replace any earlier guidance for the society's members. If something in your own circumstances is not covered here, please contact the society directly or speak with the reception team at Juniper Lane Clinic before booking.

## How the society fits with clinic visits

When a member attends Juniper Lane Clinic, the clinic records the services that were provided, the clinician who provided them, and the date of the visit. This information is what allows the society to assess a claim later. Members should always make sure that reception staff have their current details before the consultation begins, including their full name, date of birth and the member number printed on their membership card. Keeping these details accurate avoids delays when a claim is reviewed.

The clinic provides care according to clinical need, and the society's rules do not change how a clinician decides what treatment is appropriate. Membership affects the cost arrangements and the claims process, not the medical judgement involved in a consultation. Members should therefore not feel pressured to choose a service because it is covered, and should discuss all options with their clinician.

Some services at the clinic are straightforward to claim for, such as a standard consultation or a routine blood test ordered by a clinician. Others have additional conditions, which are explained in the sections below. Where a service has conditions, the clinic's reception team will normally tell members before the appointment, but members are responsible for checking that they understand what applies to them.

## Telehealth calls and repeat prescriptions

Telehealth calls are an important part of how Juniper Lane Clinic supports members who cannot easily attend in person. A telehealth call is recorded in the same way as a face-to-face consultation, and it is subject to the same general principles. Members should make sure they are in a suitable place with a reliable connection, and they should have their member number available so that the record can be matched correctly.

Repeat prescription administration is also handled through the clinic. Members who take regular medicines should ask for their repeat prescriptions in good time, and should keep track of how many items remain. The society's guidance about claims applies to the consultations and services connected with repeat prescriptions in the same way as it applies to other care, so keeping a record of what you have received is helpful.

## Child health checks, vaccinations and the travel clinic

Child health checks are a core part of the clinic's family services. These routine appointments track growth, development and general wellbeing, and they are often timed to match the recommended schedule for children at different ages. Parents and guardians should bring the child's membership details to each appointment and should check that the child is registered under the correct member.

Vaccinations are offered both as part of routine schedules and as requested services, including for travel. The travel clinic provides advice and vaccinations for people planning trips abroad, and some of these services may need to be booked well ahead of departure. Members planning travel should contact the clinic early, since some vaccines and consultations need time to arrange. The society's rules on claims apply to these services, and members should keep any receipts or statements they receive, since these can support a later claim.

Blood tests are arranged when a clinician requests them. Members will normally have the test taken at the clinic, and the results are then reviewed and shared with the patient in the usual way. Blood tests are part of many family medicine and women's health pathways, so they may arise in several different contexts.

## Women's health and physiotherapy

The women's health service at Juniper Lane Clinic covers a range of consultations and reviews that relate to reproductive and gynaecological health. These appointments are confidential, and the clinic takes care to keep records secure. Members should feel able to raise any concern they have, and the clinic's staff will explain the options available.

Physiotherapy is offered for people who need help with movement, pain or recovery from injury. A physiotherapy programme usually involves an initial assessment followed by a series of sessions, and the society's arrangements for this service are set out below.

## Minor procedures

Juniper Lane Clinic provides a number of minor procedures, which are carried out in the clinic rather than in a hospital. These may include small skin procedures, removal of minor lesions and similar interventions that a family practice can safely manage. Whether a procedure is suitable is a clinical decision, and not every patient will be a candidate for every procedure.

Larkfield Friendly Society covers minor procedures if it approves them at least 5 working days beforehand. This approval step is important. Members who arrange a minor procedure without first obtaining approval may find that the society will not cover it, even where the procedure would otherwise qualify. The clinic will normally submit the request on the member's behalf, but members should ask the reception team to confirm that the request has been sent and that the timing allows for the five working day period. Where a procedure is needed urgently, members should speak with the clinician, who can advise on the best route.

## Physiotherapy cover

Physiotherapy is covered by the society within set limits. Larkfield Friendly Society covers up to 6 physiotherapy sessions per year with a 20 percent co-payment. This means that the society meets most of the cost of each session within the annual limit, and the member pays the remaining 20 percent as a co-payment. Members should note that the limit applies per year, so planning sessions across the year can help members understand what remains available. Once the annual limit is reached, further sessions are not covered under this arrangement, and members should ask the clinic about options before they begin a course of treatment.

## Paying for care and claiming back

Larkfield Friendly Society is not billed directly, so patients pay first and claim back. This is the most important practical point in this document, and members should read it carefully. Juniper Lane Clinic does not send invoices to the society on members' behalf for the services covered by this arrangement. Instead, members pay for their care when they attend, and then submit a claim to the society to recover the amount that the society is responsible for.

This pay first and claim back approach means that members need to keep proper records. When you pay for a service, ask for a receipt or statement that shows the date, the service provided, the amount paid and the clinic's details. Keep these papers together, whether you keep them in a folder or store digital copies. The society will need this information to assess your claim, and a clear record makes the process faster for everyone.

When preparing a claim, members should include the receipt or statement, a brief description of the service, and any approval reference where one applies, such as for minor procedures. Make sure that all the details on the claim match the receipt, since differences can cause delays. If a claim is incomplete, the society may ask for further information before it can be processed, which can take additional time.

Claims must be sent within 120 days. Larkfield Friendly Society patient claims must be sent within 120 days of the date of the service or payment, whichever is applicable under the society's procedures. Members who miss this deadline may not be able to recover the cost, so it is worth submitting claims promptly after each visit. Setting a reminder when you pay for a service can help you keep track of the deadline. If you are unsure about the date that applies to your claim, contact the society before the deadline passes.

The society has no direct billing arrangement with the clinic for these services, so members should not expect the clinic to handle the claim for them. Reception staff can provide the paperwork and confirm details about the service, but the claim itself is made by the member to the society.

## Member numbers

Every member of Larkfield Friendly Society has a unique member number, which is used to identify you on all records, approvals and claims. A Larkfield Friendly Society member number has 10 digits. Members should enter the number exactly as it appears on their membership card, without spaces or additional characters, when they book appointments, complete forms or submit claims. If a number is entered incorrectly, records may not match, and this can delay both approvals and claim payments.

Members who have lost their card or who believe their number is incorrect should contact the society as soon as possible. Keep your member number safe, but remember that the clinic may ask for it when you attend, so have it available. If you are a parent or guardian arranging care for a child, make sure that the child's details are linked correctly to the appropriate membership.

## Questions and changes

If you have a question about how these rules apply to your care, please contact Juniper Lane Clinic's reception team or the society directly before you book. If your personal details change, such as your address, contact information or family arrangements, let both the clinic and the society know so that your records remain accurate. This page reflects the rules that take effect on 1 April 2026, and members should check for updates from time to time.
