# Preparing for a fasting blood test

## Why fasting matters

Some blood tests are only accurate when the body has been without food for a set period. After a meal, the level of fats, sugars and other substances in the blood rises and then falls over several hours. If a sample is taken during that rise, the result can look higher than it really is, which may lead to unnecessary worry, repeat testing or the wrong treatment decision. Fasting gives the clinician a stable baseline, so that the result reflects your normal resting state rather than what you happened to eat that morning.

Common fasting tests include checks of blood sugar, cholesterol and triglycerides, and some kidney and liver markers. Your clinician will tell you when a fasting sample is needed, and the request will usually be printed on the form you take to the laboratory or phlebotomy room. If you are not sure whether your test needs fasting, ask the reception team before you arrive, or check with your clinician when the test was booked.

Fasting is not the same as going without everything. Many people worry that they will feel faint or unwell during a fast. Most healthy adults manage well with a normal routine, provided they keep drinking plain water and avoid heavy physical exertion before the sample is taken. If you have diabetes, take regular medicines, are pregnant, or have had problems with fainting in the past, speak to your clinician before you fast, as the usual arrangements may need adjusting for you.

It also helps to understand that a fasting test is a snapshot. Your result reflects the state of your body on that particular morning, and factors such as stress, illness, poor sleep and recent exercise can all shift some values slightly. For this reason, clinicians usually look at the overall pattern of results over time rather than reading too much into a single number. Keeping your preparation consistent from one test to the next makes those comparisons more reliable.

Many patients find it useful to plan the day around the test in advance. Think about your work commitments, school runs, and any caring responsibilities, and consider whether you will be able to eat promptly afterwards. A little planning can prevent the frustration of arriving hungry for an early appointment only to find that the timing was wrong, or of rushing out of the door without the information you need.

Finally, remember that our team is here to help you prepare, not to judge you if something goes differently from the plan. Missed preparation is common, and there is a clear route to getting the test done properly. The sections below explain the practical steps, what counts as fasting and what does not, and who to contact if you are uncertain.

## The timing rule

A fasting blood test must start before 10:30 in the morning, so please book your appointment with that in mind. Arriving before the cut-off gives the phlebotomist time to take the sample, and it means your overnight fast will be as long as the instructions require. If you are offered a later slot, ask the reception team whether an earlier time is available before you accept it.

Before a fasting blood test, do not eat for 8 hours; plain water is allowed. In practice this usually means that the last meal you eat should finish at least eight hours before your appointment. For example, if your appointment is at 9:00 in the morning, you should stop eating by about 1:00 the previous afternoon, and then avoid food overnight. Some patients find it easier to have their evening meal earlier than usual, so that the eight-hour period falls comfortably during sleep.

Plain water is allowed and encouraged during the fast. Keeping well hydrated helps the phlebotomist find a vein, and it reduces the chance of feeling light-headed. Drink water normally through the fasting period, and bring a bottle with you if you will be travelling to the clinic. Sparkling water, flavoured water and soft drinks are not the same as plain water, so stick to still water unless the team tells you otherwise.

## What breaks the fast

Tea, coffee, juice, milk and chewing gum break the fast. Even a small cup of black tea or coffee can change some results, so please avoid them completely during the fasting period. Sugar-free gum can also affect the sample, so it is best left in the packet until after your test. If you are in the habit of having a morning drink to get started, plan a glass of water instead, and have your usual drink once the blood sample has been taken.

Some people are unsure about medicines, vitamins and supplements. Do not stop or change any prescribed medicine on your own. If you are unsure about a medicine, phone the nurse line, because the assistant cannot advise on medicines. The nurse can tell you whether a particular medicine should be taken with a small amount of water on the morning of the test, and whether anything should be held back. Write down the questions you want to ask before you call, including the name and strength of each medicine.

## Alcohol and other habits

No alcohol for 24 hours before a fasting blood test. Alcohol can affect several blood results, including liver tests, triglycerides and blood sugar, so the test may not reflect your usual state. Please plan the day before your appointment accordingly, especially if you are attending a social event the evening before. If you have drunk alcohol within the 24 hours before your appointment, let the phlebotomist know, as they will record it and may advise a rebooking.

Smoking is not usually part of the fasting rule, but smoking on the morning of the test can make you feel light-headed and may affect circulation. If you normally smoke, try to avoid it before the appointment and speak to your clinician if you would like support to stop.

Exercise should also be kept light before the test. A brisk walk to the clinic is fine, but heavy gym sessions or long runs on the morning of the test can change some results and may leave you feeling faint. Keep the day gentle, rest if you feel tired, and eat normally once the test is complete.

## If you eat by mistake

A patient who eats by mistake tells the phlebotomist, and the test is rebooked at no charge. Accidents happen, particularly when people are busy or have forgotten the instructions. Please do not simply skip the appointment or hide what has happened. Tell the phlebotomist as soon as you arrive, or call the clinic before your appointment if you realise that you have eaten. The team will explain the options, which usually involve rebooking for another morning when you have fasted correctly. The rebooking is free, so there is no need to worry about the cost of trying again.

If you have eaten only a very small amount, such as a single biscuit or a sip of juice, it is still important to tell the team, as they will decide whether the sample can go ahead or should be rescheduled. Be honest about what you consumed and roughly when, so that the decision can be made accurately.

## Getting your result

Fasting blood test results are ready in 3 working days. Your clinician will review the result and contact you if any action is needed, or if you should book a follow-up appointment. If you have not heard from us after this time, you can check with reception, who can confirm whether the sample has been processed and when the result is expected. Please allow the full time before calling, as the laboratory needs to complete its checks.

Results are discussed with you by your clinician, usually by phone, in person or through the secure patient messaging service where available. Please keep your contact details up to date so that we can reach you promptly. If you have questions about what a result means, make a note of them and raise them at your next appointment, or request a telephone review.

## Summary checklist

1. Book a morning appointment that starts before 10:30, and confirm that the test is a fasting test.
2. Stop eating for 8 hours before the appointment, and drink plain water as needed.
3. Avoid tea, coffee, juice, milk and chewing gum during the fast.
4. Avoid alcohol for 24 hours before the test.
5. If you are unsure about your medicines, phone the nurse line before the appointment.
6. If you eat by mistake, tell the phlebotomist straight away so the test can be rebooked at no charge.
7. Expect your result in 3 working days, and contact reception if you have not heard from us.
