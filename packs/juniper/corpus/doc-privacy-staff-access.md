# Who sees your record

## Purpose of this policy

Juniper Lane Clinic is a family practice serving the people of Port Avery. We offer family medicine, child health checks, vaccinations and a travel clinic, blood tests, physiotherapy, women's health services, minor procedures, repeat prescription administration and telehealth calls. Each of these services creates a record, and that record belongs to the person it describes. This policy explains who inside and outside the clinic may look at a record, how we check identity when someone contacts us, and what we will and will not tell callers. It applies to every member of staff, every locum and student working with us, and every contractor who has access to our systems.

## Why access is limited

Medical records contain some of the most sensitive information a person can share with anyone. People tell us about their bodies, their families, their finances and their fears, and they do so because they trust that the information will be used only to care for them. We take that trust seriously. Access to records is therefore limited to what each person needs in order to do their job on a given day. A receptionist booking an appointment does not need to read a full history, a physiotherapist does not need the detail of a travel vaccination schedule unless it bears on their treatment, and a member of the billing team needs only the information required to process a payment. Role-based permissions in our clinical system are set to reflect these needs, and they are reviewed whenever someone changes role or leaves the practice.

## Staff access and logging

Staff see only the records they need, and every access is logged. The log records who opened a record, when they opened it, and which part of the record they viewed. These logs are kept as part of our audit trail and are checked regularly. If a member of staff opens a record without a clinical or administrative reason, we treat that as a serious matter. It is investigated, and it can lead to disciplinary action up to and including dismissal, along with any reporting duty that applies under professional or data protection rules. Staff are reminded of this at induction and each year, and they must confirm that they understand these obligations.

Staff should not look up friends, family members, neighbours, colleagues or public figures out of curiosity. Staff who are themselves patients should use the same route as everyone else to access their own records, and should not use their work access to do so. Requests from patients to see their own records are handled through our formal subject access process, which is described in our separate privacy notice.

## Sharing with other services

We share information with other parts of the healthcare system when this is needed for a person's care, such as with a hospital that is treating them, a pharmacy dispensing a prescription, or a laboratory processing a blood test. We share only what the receiving service needs for that purpose. We do not share records with employers, insurers or other third parties without the patient's written consent or a legal requirement to do so. Where a legal requirement applies, we record the request, the basis for disclosure and the information released.

## Telephone identity checks

When someone calls the clinic, we must be sure we are speaking with the right person before we discuss any information. Before we share any detail from a record, staff verify the caller's name, date of birth and postcode against the record they are asking about. If the details do not match, we do not proceed, and we do not give any hint about what the record contains. Callers who are calling on behalf of someone else, such as a parent speaking for a young child or a carer speaking for an adult, must explain the relationship, and we check whether we hold an authority or consent on file before continuing. If we cannot verify the caller, we offer alternatives such as a secure message through the patient portal or a request that the patient call us directly.

These checks apply even when the caller sounds distressed, urgent or familiar. Urgency is handled by directing the caller to the right clinical route, including emergency services where a life may be at risk, rather than by skipping verification.

## What we will not confirm

Staff never confirm to a caller whether someone is a patient. This applies to every caller, including close relatives, journalists, employers, insurers and people who say they are police or other officials without presenting identification we can verify. Even the fact that a person is or is not registered with us is treated as confidential. Staff will not say whether a named individual has an appointment, has collected a prescription or has attended a service. Where a request genuinely requires a formal response, it is passed to the practice manager, who handles it under our disclosure procedure.

If you are worried about someone and want to know whether they are receiving care here, the most helpful step is to encourage them to contact us directly, or to speak with the emergency services if you believe they are at immediate risk.

## Questions and complaints

If you have a question about this policy, or you believe your information has been accessed or disclosed inappropriately, please contact the practice manager at the clinic. We will acknowledge your concern, review what happened, and tell you the outcome as far as we are able to while protecting the privacy of others. This policy takes effect on 1 February 2026 and replaces any earlier guidance on record access and telephone identity checks.
