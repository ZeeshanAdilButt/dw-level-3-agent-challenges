# Repeat prescription admin process

## Purpose and scope

This playbook sets out how the administration team at Juniper Lane Clinic in Port Avery handles repeat prescription requests from patients. It applies to all reception and admin staff who receive, check, and route repeat requests, whether they arrive by phone, in person, through the patient portal, or during a telehealth call. It is a rule for everyone in the admin team, and it takes effect from 2026-03-01.

## Processing windows

Repeat prescriptions are processed at 11:00 and 16:00 each working day. Requests received after a cut-off wait for the next processing window, unless they are handled under the same-day urgent route described below. Staff should tell patients which window their request will fall into so expectations are clear.

## Numbered steps

1. Confirm the patient's identity using two identifiers from the clinic record before accepting any request.
2. Check that the medicine appears on the patient's active repeat list. If it does not, route the request to the duty GP as a new query rather than adding it to a repeat batch.
3. Record the date and time the request was received, along with the channel used (phone, portal, in person, or telehealth).
4. Check the medicine's review status. If the patient is overdue for a review, flag the request so the clinician can decide whether a consultation is needed first.
5. Add the request to the next processing batch at 11:00 or 16:00, whichever comes first after receipt.
6. Print or prepare the batch for review. Every repeat prescription is signed by the duty GP before it is released to the patient or pharmacy.
7. Once signed, release the prescription through the agreed route and mark the request as complete in the record.
8. Notify the patient that the prescription has been sent and to which pharmacy, where the patient has chosen one.
9. Log any refusals, queries, or clinical flags against the request so the audit trail is complete.

## Same-day urgent requests

A same-day urgent prescription needs the duty GP's approval before it is issued, even when the request arrives outside a processing window. Admin staff must not issue or release an urgent prescription on their own authority. Mark the request as urgent, record the reason given by the patient, and pass it to the duty GP immediately. If the duty GP is unavailable, escalate to the practice manager and do not promise the patient a time.

## Sign-off and accountability

The duty GP signs every repeat prescription. No repeat prescription may be released without that signature, and staff must not sign on a clinician's behalf or alter a signed item. If a signed prescription later needs correction, return it to the duty GP for re-signing and record the change.

## Patient communication

Be clear and courteous. Explain the processing windows, the need for identity checks, and that some requests may be held for a review. Do not guess at clinical outcomes. If a patient is distressed or the request concerns symptoms, refer them to the duty GP or the appropriate clinical route without delay.

## Records and audit

Every request, decision, and release must be recorded in the clinic system. Records should show who handled the request, when, through which channel, and who signed the prescription. Periodic audits will check that processing windows were met, that every item carried a duty GP signature, and that urgent requests were approved before release.

## Review

This playbook is reviewed by the practice manager whenever processing arrangements change, and at least once a year. Staff who are unsure about any step should ask the practice manager before acting.
