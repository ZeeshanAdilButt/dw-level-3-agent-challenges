# Preparing for a glucose tolerance test

## About this guide

This guide explains how patients at Juniper Lane Clinic in Port Avery should prepare for a glucose tolerance test. It is written for adults and for parents or carers arranging a test on behalf of someone else. The clinic offers this test as part of its wider range of services, which include family medicine, child health checks, vaccinations, a travel clinic, blood tests, physiotherapy, women's health, minor procedures, repeat prescription administration and telehealth calls. A glucose tolerance test is a blood-based check that shows how well the body handles sugar over a short period, and your clinician may request it for reasons discussed during your appointment.

Please read this guide before your test day. Following the steps below helps the test give accurate results and avoids the need to rebook. If anything in this guide is unclear, contact the reception team during opening hours or speak with your clinician before the day of your test.

## Who should use this guide

This guide applies to anyone who has been referred for a glucose tolerance test at Juniper Lane Clinic. Some patients have taken a test before and others are attending for the first time. Either way, the preparation is the same. If you take regular medicines, including tablets, injections or inhalers, check with your clinician whether you should continue them before the test, since some medicines can affect glucose readings. Do not stop any prescribed medicine without first speaking to your clinician.

Pregnant patients, patients with a history of gastric surgery, and patients who have been unwell with vomiting or diarrhoea in the days before the test should tell the clinic at the time of booking. Your clinician may recommend a different arrangement or a rescheduled date.

## Booking your appointment

Glucose tolerance tests are offered only on set days and times. Appointments begin at either 08:00 or 08:30, and they are available Monday to Thursday only. Please do not request a Friday, weekend or public holiday slot, as these cannot be offered. When you book, the reception team will confirm which start time has been allocated to you. If you need to change your appointment, let the clinic know as early as you can so another patient can use the slot.

When booking, you will be asked for your contact details and whether you have any allergies or medical conditions that the clinical team should be aware of. Please answer these questions honestly, as they help the team plan your visit safely.

## Preparing the day before

The most important preparation step happens the evening before your test. You must fast before the test, which means avoiding all food and calorie-containing drinks for a set period. Plain water is allowed and encouraged so that you stay comfortably hydrated. Please avoid alcohol, coffee, tea with milk or sugar, fizzy drinks and fruit juice during your fasting period, as these can change your results.

Try to keep your normal routine as much as possible on the day before. Avoid unusually strenuous exercise, as heavy physical activity can affect how your body processes sugar. Get a normal night's sleep if you can, and make sure you know how you will travel to and from the clinic on the test day.

## Fasting before your test

Before a glucose tolerance test, do not eat for 10 hours. For most patients this means finishing your last meal in the early evening and then having nothing except water until you arrive at the clinic. Some patients find it easiest to eat a light, balanced evening meal and then go to bed, so that most of the fasting period passes while they sleep. If you wake during the night and feel hungry, drink water and try to rest rather than eating.

If you are unsure whether you have fasted for long enough, it is better to contact the clinic than to eat and risk an inaccurate result. The team can advise you on whether your test should go ahead or be rebooked. Remember that fasting for the full period is essential, so plan your evening meal around your arrival time rather than eating late out of habit.

## Arriving at the clinic

A glucose tolerance test cannot start late, so patients arrive 10 minutes early. Arriving on time gives the team enough time to check you in, confirm your identity and make sure you are ready to begin. If you are running late, phone the clinic as soon as you know. Please be aware that if you arrive too late to start on time, the team may not be able to proceed with the test that day, and you may need to rebook.

When you arrive, let the reception team know that you are attending for a glucose tolerance test. A member of staff will confirm that you have fasted as instructed and will explain what happens next. Bring a list of your current medicines, your appointment confirmation and any questions you want to ask your clinician.

## The test itself

The glucose tolerance test takes 2 hours from start to finish. During this time, your blood will be taken at set intervals, so it is important that you stay in the clinic for the whole period. The test uses one drink of 75 g glucose solution, which the team will give you at the start. The drink is sweet and can be unpleasant for some people, so you may wish to drink it steadily rather than all at once, as long as you finish within the time the nurse asks for. If you feel unable to finish the drink, tell the team straight away.

Patients stay at the clinic for the full 2 hours and do not eat or leave during the test. This rule protects the accuracy of your results, because eating or moving about could change your glucose levels. You are welcome to sit quietly, read, or use a phone or tablet, but please keep to the seating area and let staff know if you need to use the toilet or feel unwell. If you need a short break for a medical reason, the team will guide you on how to proceed.

## Why the days matter

Glucose tolerance tests run Monday to Thursday because samples leave on the same-day courier. The clinic cannot send samples on Friday, at the weekend or on public holidays, so the test days are chosen to make sure every sample reaches the laboratory without delay. This is also why the start times are fixed. Keeping to your allocated slot helps the clinic meet the courier collection and keeps the whole process running smoothly for every patient.

## Feeling unwell afterwards

Some people feel lightheaded, shaky, sick or unusually tired after a glucose tolerance test, particularly because of the sweet drink. Patients should arrange for someone to drive them home if they feel unwell afterwards. Please do not drive yourself if you feel dizzy, weak or unsure of your reactions. If you are alone, consider using a taxi or public transport with a companion where possible. Once you are home, eat a light snack if you feel hungry or unwell, and rest until you feel better.

If your symptoms are severe, do not wait. Contact a clinician or seek urgent care right away. The clinic can also advise by phone during opening hours, and the telehealth service can be used for follow-up questions where your clinician agrees it is suitable.

## Getting your results

Your results are not usually available on the day of the test, because samples must be analysed at the laboratory first. Your clinician will review the results and contact you to explain what they mean and what, if anything, should happen next. Do not assume a result is normal or abnormal until you have spoken with your clinician. If you have not heard back within the time your clinician has indicated, contact the reception team to check on progress.

## Questions and contact

If you have questions about preparing for a glucose tolerance test, please contact Juniper Lane Clinic in Port Avery during opening hours. You can also raise questions with your clinician at your appointment or through a telehealth call if one has been arranged. Keeping this guide with your appointment details will help you check the steps before your visit.
