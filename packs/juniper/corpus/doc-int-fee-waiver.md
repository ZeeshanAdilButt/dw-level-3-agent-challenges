# Fee waiver rules

## Purpose and scope

This playbook sets out who at Juniper Lane Clinic may waive a fee, under what conditions, and what evidence must be on file before a waiver is applied. It covers late cancellation fees, no-show fees and other administrative charges raised for family medicine, child health checks, vaccinations, the travel clinic, blood tests, physiotherapy, women's health, minor procedures, repeat prescription administration and telehealth calls.

It applies to all reception staff, practice administrators and duty managers. Clinicians do not waive fees. If a clinician believes a charge is unfair, they refer the case to reception using the steps below.

## Who may waive what

Reception may waive the first late cancellation fee for a patient in any 12 months. This is a standard courtesy and needs no sign-off beyond the reception log entry.

Reception may waive up to EUR 60 on any single charge in a waiver case. Any amount above EUR 60 goes to the duty manager for decision. Reception must never split a single charge into smaller parts to stay under the EUR 60 limit.

A hardship waiver needs form CF-30 and the clinic manager's sign-off. Hardship waivers may cover any amount, but the clinic manager's sign-off is required in every case, including where the amount is within the reception limit.

A no-show fee is waived on proof of a hospital stay or a bereavement. Proof must be seen by reception and a copy or reference noted on the patient record. Reception does not need further approval for this category.

## Numbered steps for handling a waiver request

1. Confirm the patient's identity and locate the charge on the account.
2. Check the waiver history for the past 12 months to see whether a first late cancellation waiver has already been used.
3. Identify the category of the request: first late cancellation, no-show with proof, hardship, or other.
4. For a first late cancellation, confirm no earlier waiver exists in the past 12 months, then apply the waiver and record it.
5. For a no-show, ask the patient for proof of a hospital stay or a bereavement. Record the proof reference on the account before applying the waiver.
6. For any other request, check the amount. If it is EUR 60 or less, reception may approve. If it is above EUR 60, pass the case to the duty manager with the account reference and reason.
7. For a hardship request, give the patient form CF-30 to complete. Do not apply the waiver until the completed form has been received.
8. Send the completed CF-30 to the clinic manager for sign-off. Do not apply the waiver until sign-off is recorded.
9. Once approved, apply the waiver to the account and add a note stating the category, the amount waived and the approver.
10. Inform the patient of the outcome in plain language, including any remaining balance and how to pay it.
11. Where a waiver is refused, explain the reason in general terms and point the patient to the complaints route if they disagree.
12. File all supporting documents with the account record. Do not keep copies at reception desks.

## Recording and audit

Every waiver, approved or refused, must be logged the same day. The log entry must include the patient reference, the category, the amount, the decision, the name of the person who made it, and any proof reference. Duty managers review the log each week and report any pattern of repeated waivers to the clinic manager.

Waivers applied outside these rules are treated as a breach of this playbook and must be reported to the clinic manager. Staff who are unsure whether a request falls within reception's authority should ask the duty manager before acting, not after.

## Patient communication

Staff should explain fee decisions calmly and without judgement. Avoid implying that a patient is at fault for a cancellation or missed appointment. Where a patient is in financial difficulty, offer the hardship route and explain what the form involves, without asking for details beyond what the form requires.

## Review

This playbook takes effect on 1 March 2026. The clinic manager reviews it at least once a year, or sooner if the fee schedule changes or an audit finds a gap.
