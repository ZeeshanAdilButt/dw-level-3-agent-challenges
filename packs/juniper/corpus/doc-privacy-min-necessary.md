# Asking only for what we need

## Purpose

This policy sets out the personal information that Juniper Lane Clinic asks for when a patient books an appointment, makes an enquiry, or uses our telehealth service. It applies to all clinicians, reception staff, and the online and telephone assistant that helps patients book and ask questions. It takes effect on 2026-02-01 and replaces any earlier guidance on booking information.

## The principle

Our standard is simple: staff and the assistant ask only for the minimum data needed for the task in hand. We do not collect information because it might be useful someday. Each question we ask should have a clear reason tied to the booking, the enquiry, or the safe delivery of care. If a detail is not needed for that purpose, we do not ask for it.

## What a booking needs

A booking only needs the patient's name or ID number, the date of birth for verification, the service required, and a preferred time. The date of birth is used to confirm we are booking the right person, particularly where two patients share a name. The service tells us which clinician, room, or equipment to prepare, such as a child health check, a vaccination, a blood test, a physiotherapy session, or a telehealth call. The preferred time lets us offer the nearest suitable slot.

Nothing beyond these items is required to hold an appointment. Where a clinician later needs more detail for clinical care, they will ask for it at the appropriate point, and the patient may decline to answer anything they are not comfortable sharing, although this may affect the care we can safely provide.

## No medical reason needed to book

Patients do not have to give a medical reason to book. Whether someone is booking a routine review, a travel vaccination, or a follow-up, they may simply choose the service from the list. Reception and the assistant will not press for a diagnosis, symptoms, or an explanation before offering a time. If a clinician needs to understand the purpose of a visit to plan it properly, they may ask during the appointment itself or through a short triage conversation, but that question is never a condition of booking.

Where a patient volunteers a reason, staff will use it only to choose the right service, clinician, or urgency level.

## Questions and the assistant

When a patient asks a general question through the assistant or by phone, the assistant asks only for what it needs to answer. Often that is nothing at all, because general information about opening arrangements, our services, or how to prepare for a test does not require identifying anyone. If a question concerns a specific record or prescription, the assistant asks for the minimum verification needed to protect that information and then passes the matter to the appropriate member of staff.

The assistant will not ask for payment details, national identifiers, or other sensitive information unless a specific, approved process requires them, and it will say why when it does.

## Keeping records lean

Staff should record only what is needed to deliver the service and meet our legal obligations. When information is no longer needed, it should be handled according to our data retention procedures. Any staff member who notices more information being collected than this policy allows should raise it with the practice manager.

## Questions and review

Patients with concerns about what we ask for can speak to reception or contact the practice manager. This policy will be reviewed annually, or sooner if our services or the law change.
