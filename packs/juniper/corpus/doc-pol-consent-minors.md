# Consent for children and young people

## Purpose

This policy sets out how Juniper Lane Clinic in Port Avery seeks and records consent for patients who are children or young people. It applies to all family medicine, child health checks, vaccinations, travel clinic appointments, blood tests, physiotherapy, women's health services, minor procedures, repeat prescription administration and telehealth calls.

## Who gives consent

Consent is sought from the patient wherever they are able to understand the choice being offered. Where a child is not able to make the decision alone, a parent or legal guardian is asked to give consent on their behalf, and the clinician will explain the proposed care in terms the child can follow.

Patients aged 16 and over may attend alone. Clinicians assess each young person's understanding and will involve a parent or guardian only with the young person's agreement, unless there is a safeguarding concern or a legal requirement to do so.

## Chaperones

A chaperone is offered at every intimate examination. The patient may accept or decline the chaperone, and the clinician will record the choice in the patient's notes. A chaperone may be a member of clinical staff trained for the role, and a patient may ask for a particular chaperone to be present where this is reasonably possible.

## Recording consent

Clinicians record the consent given, the person who gave it, and any limits the patient or guardian placed on the care. Consent may be withdrawn at any time before or during treatment, and the clinician will explain the consequences of withdrawal.

## Questions and concerns

Patients and families who have questions about this policy can speak to any clinician or the practice reception team.
