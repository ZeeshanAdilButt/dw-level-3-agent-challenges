# Preparing for a minor procedure

## About this guide

This guide explains how to prepare for a minor procedure at Juniper Lane Clinic in Port Avery. Minor procedures are short treatments carried out in a consulting room, such as the removal of a skin lesion, the drainage of a small cyst, or the removal of a foreign body from the skin. They are performed under local anaesthetic, which numbs the area so that you feel pressure but not sharp pain. Most people go home the same day and return to normal activities within a few days, although the area may remain tender for longer.

Your clinician will decide whether a minor procedure is suitable for you. This decision depends on the size and position of the area to be treated, your general health, any medicines you take, and whether you have allergies. If you have a bleeding disorder, are taking blood thinning medicines, or have a weakened immune system, tell the clinic before your appointment so that the plan can be adjusted. Please bring a list of all current medicines, including those bought without a prescription and any herbal remedies.

Many people feel anxious before a procedure, and this is normal. Asking questions beforehand helps. You can ask about what the clinician will do, how long the appointment will take, what the anaesthetic will feel like, and what to expect afterwards. If you are unsure about any part of the process, contact the front desk and ask to speak with the clinician who will carry out the treatment. Telehealth calls are available if you want to discuss your concerns before attending in person.

If the area to be treated is on a child, a young person, or an adult who needs support, a parent, carer or trusted adult may attend with them. Please let the reception team know in advance so that arrangements can be made.

## Before your appointment

1. Read this guide in full and note any questions you would like to ask.
2. Make a list of your current medicines and any allergies, and bring it with you.
3. Tell us if you have had a reaction to a local anaesthetic in the past.
4. Arrange for someone to take you home, because you should not drive yourself after the procedure.
5. Plan to take time off work or other commitments for the rest of the day.
6. Avoid starting new blood thinning medicines or supplements unless your clinician has advised this.
7. Wear loose clothing that gives access to the area. Tight collars, sleeves or waistbands can be difficult to manage and may rub the treated site.
8. Arrive a few minutes early so that you are not rushed.

## At your pre-check

Before the day of the procedure, you will have a short pre-check appointment with a clinician. This is when your medical history is confirmed, the treatment plan is explained, and any risks are discussed. You will be asked to read and sign a consent form. Written consent is signed at the pre-check, not on the day of the procedure, so that you have time to read the information and ask questions in a calm setting. You are free to change your mind at any point before the treatment begins.

Please keep the consent form and bring any questions that arise during the pre-check to your appointment.

## On the day of the procedure

1. Check in at reception and confirm your name and date of birth.
2. Wait in the waiting area until you are called to the consulting room.
3. The clinician will examine the area, mark it if needed, and clean the skin.
4. A local anaesthetic will be given. You may feel a brief sting followed by numbness.
5. The procedure will be carried out. You may feel pressure or pulling, but you should not feel sharp pain. Tell the clinician if you feel discomfort at any time.
6. Dressings will be applied to the site. Follow the instructions you are given about when to change them.
7. Removed tissue is sent to the laboratory, and results take 10 working days (ten working days) to arrive. A clinician will contact you about the results, or you can ask at reception how you will receive them.
8. An aftercare leaflet is handed over on the day of the procedure. Read it before you leave and keep it somewhere easy to find.
9. Before you leave, make sure you have a lift home. Patients should arrange a lift home after a minor procedure, because the local anaesthetic may affect your concentration and co-ordination for a few hours.

## After you go home

Keep the dressing clean and dry for the period described in your aftercare leaflet. Look at the site each day for signs of infection, including increasing redness, swelling, heat, pus or a fever. If you notice any of these, or if bleeding does not stop with gentle pressure, contact the clinic or seek urgent care. For questions about aftercare, call the clinic during opening hours or arrange a telehealth call with a clinician.
