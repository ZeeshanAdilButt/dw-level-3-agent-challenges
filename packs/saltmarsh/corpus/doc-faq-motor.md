# FAQ: motor insurance

This FAQ answers common questions about motor cover from Saltmarsh Mutual. It applies from 1 July 2026. Saltmarsh Mutual is owned by its policyholders, so the answers below are written to explain how your cover works in plain terms. If your policy schedule or policy wording says something different, the policy wording applies.

## About our motor products

**What motor products do you offer?**

We offer two motor policies: Motor Third Party and Motor Comprehensive. Both can be extended with optional riders, including cover for accidental damage, legal expenses, home emergency and a courtesy car, where these are available on your policy.

**What is the difference between Motor Third Party and Motor Comprehensive?**

Motor Third Party covers only your legal liability to other people and their property. It does not pay to repair or replace your own vehicle if it is damaged. Motor Comprehensive covers your own vehicle as well as your legal liability to others, subject to the terms, excesses and exclusions in your policy.

**Does Motor Third Party cover my own car if I have an accident?**

No. Motor Third Party covers only your legal liability to other people and their property. If you want cover for your own vehicle, you would need to consider Motor Comprehensive or an appropriate rider.

## Excesses

**What is an excess?**

An excess is the amount you pay towards a claim before we pay the rest. Excesses vary by policy and by type of claim.

**What is the windscreen replacement excess on Motor Comprehensive?**

The windscreen replacement excess on Motor Comprehensive is £85. This is the amount you pay when you make a windscreen claim, and it applies in addition to any other excess that may be relevant to your policy.

**What is the theft excess on Motor Comprehensive?**

The Motor Comprehensive theft excess is £300. This is the amount you pay towards a claim where your vehicle has been stolen or an attempt has been made to steal it, subject to the terms of your policy.

## Driving other cars

**Can I drive other cars under my policy?**

Driving other cars cover is for policyholders aged 25 or over. If you are 25 or over and your policy includes this cover, you may drive other vehicles in the ways set out in your policy. If you are under 25, this cover is not available to you, so please check your policy schedule before driving a vehicle that is not your own.

**Who is covered when I drive someone else's car?**

Cover depends on the terms of your policy and the circumstances of the journey. Your policy wording sets out the conditions that apply, including who is permitted to drive and any limits on the use of the vehicle.

## No-claims discount

**How does the no-claims discount work?**

The no-claims discount rewards you for years without making a claim. Discounts increase with each claim-free year, up to a maximum level.

**What is the maximum no-claims discount?**

Six or more years without a claim earn the maximum 70% no-claims discount. Your discount is applied to your premium at renewal, and the exact amount depends on your claims history and the terms of your policy.

**Does making a claim affect my no-claims discount?**

It can. Making a claim may reduce or remove your no-claims discount, depending on the type of claim and the terms of your policy. If you are unsure how a particular claim would affect your discount, please contact us before making it.

## Cooling-off period

**Can I cancel my motor policy after I buy it?**

Yes, within the cooling-off period. The cooling-off period on a motor policy is 14 days, starting from the date you receive your policy documents or the date your cover starts, whichever your policy wording specifies. If you cancel within this period, we will refund the premium you have paid, less any amount that relates to cover you have already used, in line with the terms of your policy.

**What happens if I want to cancel after the cooling-off period?**

You can still cancel at any time. Cancellation after the cooling-off period may mean that a refund is calculated on a pro-rata basis, and an administration charge may apply. Please see your policy wording for details.

## Making a claim

**How do I report a motor claim?**

You should tell us as soon as reasonably possible after an incident. You can report a claim through the channels shown in your policy documents. Please have your policy number, the date and details of the incident, and any information about other parties involved to hand.

**What happens after I report a claim?**

We will record the claim, confirm what cover applies and explain any next steps, including whether repairs need to be arranged or whether we need further information from you. We will keep you informed until the claim is settled.

**Do I need to tell the police about an accident or theft?**

You should report theft to the police and obtain a crime reference number where possible. For accidents involving injury or significant damage to other parties' property, please follow the guidance in your policy and the requirements of the law where you are.

## Other questions

**Where can I find my policy wording?**

Your policy documents, including your schedule and policy wording, set out the full terms of your cover. If you cannot find them, please contact us and we will help you locate them.

**What should I do if my circumstances change?**

Please tell us about changes that may affect your cover, such as a new vehicle, a change of address or a change to who drives your vehicle. Failing to tell us about relevant changes could affect your cover or any claim you make.
