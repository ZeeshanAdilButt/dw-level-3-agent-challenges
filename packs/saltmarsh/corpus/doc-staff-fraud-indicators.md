# Fraud indicators

## Purpose and scope

This playbook sets out the indicators that claims handlers at Saltmarsh Mutual use to identify possible fraud across Home Essentials, Home Plus, Motor Third Party and Motor Comprehensive policies, including the optional riders for accidental damage, legal expenses, home emergency and courtesy car. It is a guide for staff, not a finding that any individual is dishonest. An indicator is a reason to look more closely, and most flagged claims turn out to be genuine.

## Red flags to watch for

Handlers should consider a claim for closer review when one or more of the following features is present:

- The loss occurs very soon after the policy was taken out or altered. A claim made within 60 days of the policy start date is flagged for review in every case, regardless of the apparent size of the loss.
- The customer is reluctant to provide receipts, photographs, repair estimates or access to the damaged property or vehicle.
- The description of the incident changes between the first notice of loss and later conversations, or does not match the physical evidence.
- The value claimed is high relative to the item or vehicle, or appears to have been inflated after an initial estimate.
- Documents look altered, are inconsistent in formatting or dates, or cannot be verified with the issuing party.
- Third parties involved in the loss have links to the customer that were not declared, or they consistently decline to give details.
- The loss is reported in circumstances where independent witnesses, police reports or other records would normally exist but none can be produced.
- The customer pushes for rapid settlement without allowing the normal enquiries to take place.

No single indicator should decide the outcome. Handlers should record which indicators they observed, what they did about each one, and the reasons for their decision in the claim notes.

## Referral rule

A customer who has made three or more claims in 24 months triggers a referral to the special investigations unit. The count includes claims across all of the policies and riders held by the same customer and any linked household member, and it applies whether or not earlier claims were paid. Handlers should check claim history at first notice of loss so the trigger is identified early.

Separately, any claim that has been flagged for review must be referred to the special investigations unit within 2 working days of the flag being raised. Handlers must not wait for the outcome of their own preliminary enquiries before making this referral, and the flag date should be recorded on the claim file so the timing can be audited.

## What to do after a referral

Once a claim is referred, the handler should keep the customer informed in plain terms that additional checks are being made, without suggesting that fraud has been proven. Ordinary service standards continue to apply, including acknowledgement of the claim and any statutory timescales. Handlers must not discuss suspicions with third parties and must not contact the customer about the investigation except as agreed with the special investigations unit.

## Internal reference

Operation Harbour-12

Staff with questions about the indicators in this playbook should raise them with their team leader in the first instance, who can escalate to the special investigations unit where needed.
