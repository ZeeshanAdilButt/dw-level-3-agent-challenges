# Home policy wording: how to read it

## Purpose of this document

This document explains how to read the home policy wording issued by Saltmarsh Mutual. It is written for policyholders, prospective members and anyone who wants to understand what a home policy covers and how its parts fit together. It does not replace the policy documents themselves.

## Which products the wording covers

The current home policy wording is coded HW-2026-07. The home wording HW-2026-07 applies to both Home Essentials and Home Plus. Home Essentials carries the product code HOM-ESS-1, and Home Plus carries the product code HOM-PLS-2. Where a clause in this document or in your policy names a product, it refers to these codes, so you can check which product your own policy is written under.

Both products are sold to members as part of our mutual structure, which means that policyholders own the company. The wording is the same for both products, but the schedule that comes with your policy sets out the sums insured, the excesses and any optional riders you have chosen.

## When the July 2026 wordings apply

The July 2026 wordings apply to policies that start or renew on or after 1 July 2026. If your policy started or last renewed before that date, the wording that was in force at that time continues to govern your cover until your next renewal. Your schedule shows the wording code that applies to your policy, so you can confirm which version you hold.

## How the schedule and the wording fit together

Your policy is made up of two parts that must be read together. The wording, coded HW-2026-07, sets out the general terms, definitions, exclusions and conditions that apply to all home policies of its type. The schedule is the personal document issued to you, and it states the details that belong to your home and your choices, such as the address insured, the sums insured, any excess you have agreed to pay and the optional riders you have bought.

Where the schedule states a specific figure or item, the schedule overrides the wording for that item. For example, if the wording describes a general limit for a category of contents and your schedule states a different agreed amount for a named item, the schedule amount applies. If something is not mentioned in the schedule, the wording governs it.

## Reading definitions and exclusions

Words with a defined meaning are capitalised in the wording and listed in the definitions section. Read these carefully, because a term such as "Buildings" or "Contents" may have a narrower or broader meaning than in everyday speech. Exclusions set out what the policy does not cover. They are not hidden traps: they tell you where the cover ends so that you can arrange other protection if you need it.

## Optional riders

Riders extend or adjust the cover in the wording. They may include protection for accidental damage, cover for legal expenses, a home emergency service or other benefits. A rider applies only if it is shown in your schedule. Where a rider and the wording conflict, the rider governs for the matters it addresses.

## Making a claim

If something happens that you think may be covered, tell us as soon as you reasonably can. Your first notice of loss starts the claims process, and we will guide you through the steps from there to settlement. Keep records of the damage, and take reasonable steps to prevent further loss. The conditions section of the wording explains what is expected of you when you make a claim.

## Questions

If any part of the wording is unclear, contact us before relying on it. We would rather explain a clause than have you discover a gap in your cover when you need it.
