# Subsidence protocol

## Purpose and scope

This protocol sets out how Saltmarsh Mutual handles reported subsidence on insured homes, from first notice of loss through to settlement. It applies to all claims handlers, surveyors and claims managers working on Home Essentials and Home Plus claims, and to any related claim where a home emergency or accidental damage rider is in play. Read it alongside the standing claims handling guide.

## Initial triage

When a policyholder reports cracking, movement or sticking doors and windows, the handler should record the date, the location of each crack and a description of any changes since the last inspection. Where a crack is wider than 3 mm, the handler must arrange a monitoring survey before any repair is authorised. A crack of 3mm or less is recorded and reviewed at the next scheduled contact, unless other signs of movement are present.

## Monitoring

Where a monitoring survey is required, subsidence monitoring runs for 12 months from the date the first tell-tale or gauge is fitted. Surveyors should take readings at the intervals set out in the surveyor brief and log each reading against the claim reference. Handlers should not close the file, nor agree a settlement figure, until the 12 months of readings are complete or a qualified structural engineer has given written sign-off that movement has stopped.

## Reserves and authority

Initial reserves should reflect the worst reasonable outcome on the evidence held at the time, and must be reviewed after each monitoring reading. Any reserve increase above the authority limit in the claims delegation schedule must be referred to the claims manager. Keep all reserve notes factual and tied to the survey evidence.

## Customer communication

Explain to the policyholder what is being monitored, why, and how long it will take. Avoid speculating on cause or cost in writing before the engineer's findings are available.
