# Escape of water claims: what is covered

This guide explains how Saltmarsh Mutual handles claims for water damage in your home. It is written for policyholders who have had a burst or leak and want to know what happens next. Please read it alongside your policy wording, particularly clause 4.2 (escape of water) and the general exclusions.

## What is covered

Clause 4.2(b) covers damage caused by water escaping from a burst or leaking fixed water pipe inside the home. A fixed water pipe is part of the plumbing system of the property, such as the supply and waste pipes that run through floors, walls, ceilings and roof spaces. Damage to the building and to your contents can both fall within this cover, subject to the terms of your plan and any riders you hold.

The escape of water limit is £25,000 per claim. This is the most we will pay for damage from a single escape of water event, including repairs to the affected structure and any replacement of damaged contents that are covered under your plan. If the cost of the damage would exceed this limit, the excess is not payable under the claim.

The cost of finding the source of a leak is covered up to £2,500. Leaks are often hidden behind walls, under floors or beneath tiles, and opening up the property to locate the fault can be costly. This cost is included within the same clause, and it is subject to the £2,500 limit for finding the source of a leak. Repairs to the pipe itself are assessed separately under the terms of the policy.

## What you need to do after a leak

Prompt action limits the damage and protects your claim. After a leak, you must turn off the water at the stopcock and tell Saltmarsh without delay. If you do not know where your stopcock is, ask a plumber or check your home information pack. Turning off the supply is one of the most effective steps you can take to limit further damage while the leak is being investigated.

More generally, after any home loss you must take sensible steps to stop further damage. This may include moving belongings away from water, drying affected areas where it is safe to do so, and arranging temporary protection for damaged roofs or windows. You should keep receipts for any reasonable costs you incur in doing this, as these may be recoverable under your policy. Sensible steps do not mean putting yourself at risk, and you should not attempt electrical or structural work that is unsafe.

## Making a claim

You can report a leak or burst to Saltmarsh by phone or through your online account. Please give us as much detail as you can, including when the problem was noticed, where the water is coming from, and what action you have already taken. We will then arrange for the claim to be assessed, which may include a visit from an approved contractor to locate the source of the leak and carry out repairs.

Keep photographs of the damage, the affected areas and any plumbing work carried out. If you are unsure whether your situation is covered, contact us before having work done, as this helps us settle the claim quickly and fairly.
