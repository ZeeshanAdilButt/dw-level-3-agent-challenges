# How claims are settled

## Purpose and scope

This document explains how Saltmarsh Mutual settles claims under its home and motor policies. It applies to claims made under Home Essentials, Home Plus, Motor Third Party and Motor Comprehensive, and to claims made under the optional riders for accidental damage, legal expenses, home emergency and a courtesy car. It covers the stages a claim passes through, from the first notice of loss to the point where the settlement is paid. It does not change the terms of any policy. Where a policy wording and this document appear to differ, the policy wording applies.

## Notifying a claim

A claim begins when a policyholder tells us about an event that may be covered. This is the first notice of loss. Notice can be given through our online account area, through the app, by phone or in writing. The more detail a policyholder gives at this stage, the faster we can decide the next steps. Useful details include the date and time of the event, what happened, what was damaged or lost, any other parties involved, and any photographs, receipts or police reference numbers that are available.

Once notice is received, we confirm the claim, allocate a reference and tell the policyholder what we need next. Some claims need a specialist, such as a loss adjuster, a repairer or a legal adviser, and we will say so as early as we can. Policyholders should avoid making permanent repairs before we have seen the damage, except where it is necessary to prevent further loss, in which case the cost of reasonable emergency steps is normally considered as part of the claim.

## Assessing the claim

After notice is given, we check that the event is covered by the policy and that the policy was in force when it happened. We then look at the cause of the loss, the extent of the damage and the value of what was lost or damaged. For home claims this may involve an inspection of the property. For motor claims it may involve an inspection of the vehicle, a review of repair estimates and information from any other parties involved.

We may ask for further information, such as proof of ownership, receipts, valuations or statements. A request for information does not mean a claim has been refused. If we cannot reach a decision on the information available, we will say what is still outstanding and why it is needed. Where a claim is declined in whole or in part, we will explain the reasons in writing and set out how the policyholder can raise a complaint.

## How we settle

Once a claim is accepted, we settle it in one of three ways: by repair, by replacement or by cash payment. The method depends on the type of loss, the policy the claim falls under, and what is practical. Where a claim is settled by repair, we arrange or pay for the work to be carried out. Where it is settled by replacement, we provide or pay for an item of equivalent type and quality. Where it is settled by cash payment, we pay an amount to the policyholder that reflects the value of the loss.

Saltmarsh may choose to repair or replace an item instead of paying cash. We make this choice on the basis of what will put the policyholder back in the position they were in before the loss, as far as that is reasonable. We will tell the policyholder which option we are taking and why. Where a policyholder has a preference, we will consider it, but the final decision on the method of settlement rests with us under the terms of the policy.

Repairs are carried out by repairers approved by us or by repairers chosen by the policyholder, where the policy allows. We check the quality of work and the cost of any repair before we pay for it. If a repair reveals further damage that was caused by the same event, we will include that damage in the claim.

Replacement items are chosen to match the original as closely as is reasonably practicable. Where an item is no longer made, we will offer the nearest equivalent. We will not normally replace an item with one of higher specification unless that is the only practical option.

For cash settlements, the amount is based on the cost of repair or replacement, or on the value of the item at the time of the loss, depending on the policy terms. We will explain how the figure was reached.

## Agreeing the amount

Once we have assessed the loss and decided the method of settlement, we will confirm the amount in writing. This is the agreed settlement. If the policyholder accepts the amount, they should tell us so. If they do not agree, they can provide further evidence or ask for the assessment to be reviewed. We aim to resolve disagreements through discussion first, and we will keep the policyholder informed while a review is in progress.

Where an independent expert is needed to resolve a difference in view about the amount, we will explain how that process works, including any costs that may apply. Under no circumstances will a policyholder be asked to accept an amount they do not agree with in order to receive any part of the settlement that is not in dispute.

## Payment of settlements

When the settlement is agreed, we pay it. An agreed settlement is paid within 5 working days. Where a settlement is made in cash, the payment is made to the policyholder. Where the settlement is made by repair or replacement, we pay the repairer or supplier directly once the work is complete and we have checked it, or we pay the policyholder if they have arranged the work themselves and provided the necessary invoices.

Settlements are paid by bank transfer. We need the policyholder to have a valid bank account on record. If the account details change, the policyholder should tell us before the settlement is due, so that payment is not delayed. We will never ask for account details by unsolicited phone call or email, and policyholders should be cautious of any request that appears to come from us but does not follow our normal procedures.

Interim payments may be made where a claim is large or will take a long time to complete. An interim payment reduces the final settlement by the amount already paid.

## Excess

Most policies carry an excess, which is the part of each claim the policyholder pays themselves. Where a settlement is made, the excess is taken off the settlement amount. The excess that applies is shown in the policy schedule. Where more than one item is claimed under the same event, the excess normally applies once, unless the policy says otherwise. Where a claim is settled by repair or replacement, the excess is deducted from the amount we pay to the repairer or supplier, and any balance is then due from the policyholder to the repairer or supplier.
