# Claims handling playbook

## Purpose and scope

This playbook sets out how Saltmarsh Mutual handlers take a claim from first notice of loss through to settlement. It applies to all home and motor claims, including claims made under the optional riders for accidental damage, legal expenses, home emergency and courtesy car. Handlers must follow it unless a senior handler has approved a documented exception. It is an internal document for staff only and must not be shared with customers, brokers or third parties.

## Claim tiers

Every claim is assigned to one of three tiers when it is registered, based on the estimated settlement value recorded at intake. The tier determines the routing, the first contact target and the level of authority needed to settle.

Tier 1 claims are those under £2,500 and are fast-tracked. They may be settled by any qualified handler within their authority, and the fast-track route skips the second-review step where the file is otherwise complete.

Tier 2 claims sit between the Tier 1 and Tier 3 thresholds. They follow the standard route with full file review before any offer is made.

Tier 3 claims are those over £25,000 and go to a senior handler. No offer above the standard authority may be made on a Tier 3 claim without senior handler sign-off recorded on the file.

Where the estimate changes during the claim, the tier must be re-assessed and updated on the system. A claim that moves across a threshold is routed to the appropriate handler on the day the change is recorded.

## First contact

Handlers should make first contact with the policyholder within 1 working day on Tier 1 claims. First contact means a call, a secure message or a letter that acknowledges the claim, confirms the next steps and records any urgent needs, such as temporary accommodation or a replacement vehicle under the courtesy car rider. For Tier 2 and Tier 3 claims, the first contact standard is set by the team lead and recorded in the team's service schedule.

If first contact cannot be made within the target, the handler must log the attempts and the reason on the file and notify the team lead the same day.

## Settlement and records

Settlement decisions must be supported by the evidence on file, including photographs, estimates, police or fire references where relevant, and any engineer or loss adjuster reports. Each settlement must be recorded with the reason for the amount paid and the authority under which it was approved. Notes must be factual and written so that another handler could pick up the file without asking questions.

Where a rider is relevant, such as legal expenses or home emergency cover, the handler must confirm cover before instructing any supplier or incurring costs, and record the confirmation on the file.

## Escalation and complaints

Any claim where the policyholder disputes a decision, where a deadline is at risk, or where the handler believes the claim may be fraudulent must be escalated to the team lead. Potential fraud is referred to the counter-fraud team without tipping off the policyholder. Complaints must be logged in the complaints system within one working day of receipt and handled under the complaints procedure.
