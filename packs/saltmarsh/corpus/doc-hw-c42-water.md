# Clause 4.2: escape of water

## Purpose and scope

This clause sets out when Saltmarsh Mutual will cover loss caused by water escaping inside the home. It applies to the Home Essentials and Home Plus policies. It should be read together with the policy schedule, the general conditions and the definitions section, which explain how terms such as "home", "policyholder" and "excess" are used. Where this clause and the schedule differ, the schedule shows the amounts that apply to your policy.

Escape of water is one of the most common causes of home claims. Water can damage floors, walls, ceilings, furniture, carpets and stored belongings, often before the leak is noticed. This clause explains which sources of water are covered, what the limits are, and what you must do if a leak happens. Reading it before you need it will help you act quickly and keep your claim straightforward.

## Scope of cover under clause 4.2

Clause 4.2 is divided into three sub clauses. Each covers a different source of water, and each is subject to the same limits and conditions set out below. The sub clauses are summarised here so you can see at a glance which one may apply to your situation, and the detailed wording follows in the numbered steps that come later in this clause.

1. Clause 4.2(a) covers escape of water from a domestic appliance inside the home, such as a washing machine or dishwasher.
2. Clause 4.2(b) covers damage caused by water escaping from a burst or leaking fixed water pipe inside the home.
3. Clause 4.2(c) covers the reasonable costs of finding the source of a leak where the leak itself is covered under 4.2(a) or 4.2(b), subject to the tracing limit described below.

## Limits and costs

The escape of water limit is £25,000 per claim. This is the most Saltmarsh Mutual will pay for the damage from any one escape of water event, before any excess shown in your policy schedule is deducted. If the cost of repairing and replacing damaged property is more than this figure, the difference is not covered under this clause.

Finding the source of a leak can be expensive, because it may involve lifting floorboards, opening walls or using specialist equipment. Under clause 4.2(c), the cost of finding the source of a leak is covered up to £2,500 per claim. This sum is part of the overall escape of water limit and is not in addition to it. Tracing costs are only payable where the leak falls within clause 4.2(a) or 4.2(b).

It is important to understand what this clause does not pay for. The cost of repairing the burst pipe itself is not covered, only the damage it causes. For example, if a pipe bursts behind a kitchen wall, Saltmarsh Mutual will pay to repair the damaged plaster, flooring and kitchen units, but will not pay to replace the pipe. The same principle applies to a faulty appliance: the cost of repairing or replacing the appliance is not covered under this clause, only the resulting damage to the home and its contents.

## Exclusions

Clause 4.3 excludes damage from a leak that has gone on for 14 days or more. This exclusion exists because long-running leaks are usually the result of neglect or a failure to notice and report a problem, and they tend to cause progressive damage that could have been limited if the leak had been dealt with earlier. Where a leak has continued for fourteen days or more before it is reported and stopped, Saltmarsh Mutual will not pay for the damage caused during that period. Clause 4.3 should be read in full, as it contains further detail about how the duration of a leak is assessed.

## Claims process after a leak

If you discover a leak, the steps below will protect your position and help us handle your claim promptly. Following them is a condition of cover under this clause.

1. Stop the water escaping as quickly as you safely can. If the leak comes from an appliance, switch it off at the wall and turn off its water supply valve if one is fitted.
2. Protect the property and your belongings by moving items away from the water and placing buckets or towels where needed. Keep receipts for any reasonable emergency costs you incur.
3. Take clear photographs and videos of the damage and of the source of the leak where it is visible, before anything is cleaned up or repaired. This evidence helps us assess the claim accurately.
4. Contact Saltmarsh Mutual to report the loss as soon as you can. Early reporting allows us to arrange assessment, reduce further damage and advise on the next steps.
5. Keep any damaged items you can, and do not throw them away until an assessor has seen them, unless they present a health hazard.
6. Cooperate with any tracing or repair work we arrange, and provide invoices and records of any costs you have paid.

After a leak you must turn off the water at the stopcock and tell Saltmarsh without delay. The stopcock is the main valve that controls the water supply to your home, and turning it off stops the flow from all fixed pipes at once, which is usually the quickest way to prevent further damage. If you are unsure where the stopcock is, check your property documents or ask a plumber to show you. Failing to turn off the water or to report the leak promptly may affect the amount we are able to pay under this clause.
