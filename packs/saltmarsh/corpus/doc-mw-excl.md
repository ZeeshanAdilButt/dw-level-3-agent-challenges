# Motor policy wording: general exclusions

## Purpose of this section

This section sets out the general exclusions that apply to all motor cover sold by Saltmarsh Mutual, including Motor Third Party and Motor Comprehensive, and to any optional riders attached to a motor policy. An exclusion describes a loss, cost or type of use that the policy does not pay for. Exclusions should be read together with the insuring clauses, the definitions and the conditions of the policy schedule, which together determine what is covered and what is not.

## How to read these exclusions

The steps below explain how the exclusions are applied when a claim is made. They are numbered so that each stage can be referred to separately.

1. Identify the insured vehicle and the policyholder named in the current schedule.
2. Confirm the cover level held, whether Motor Third Party or Motor Comprehensive, and any riders added.
3. Establish the circumstances of the loss, including the date, time, place and the person driving at the time.
4. Check each general exclusion in this section against those circumstances.
5. Where an exclusion applies, the affected part of the claim is declined, although other parts of the same claim may still be considered under the policy.
6. Where more than one exclusion could apply, each is assessed separately and the decision is recorded with reasons.

## Wear, tear and mechanical condition

The policy does not cover damage to the tyres caused by normal wear, by punctures, or by braking. Replacement of worn tyres, repair of punctures and the cost of brake wear are maintenance costs and remain the responsibility of the policyholder. Where a tyre is damaged in an accident that is otherwise covered, the damage caused by that accident may be considered, but the underlying wear or puncture is not.

The policy also does not cover loss caused by a mechanical or electrical fault that a valid MOT certificate would have identified at the time of the most recent test. This exclusion is intended to encourage the upkeep of roadworthy vehicles. Saltmarsh Mutual may ask for a copy of the MOT certificate and the test record when a claim involves a fault of this kind, and will consider the evidence in deciding whether the exclusion applies.

## Impairment of the driver

Loss that occurs while the driver is over the legal alcohol limit for driving in the place where the loss happened is not covered. Loss that occurs while the driver is unfit to drive through the use of drugs, whether prescribed or not, is also not covered, except where the driver is taking a medicine in accordance with medical advice and the medicine does not impair driving. Saltmarsh Mutual may ask for evidence of the driver's condition, such as a test result or a police record, and will treat a refusal to provide reasonable evidence as relevant to the decision.

## Undeclared modifications

The policy does not cover loss involving a modification to the vehicle that was not declared to Saltmarsh Mutual before the loss occurred. A modification includes any change to the engine, bodywork, suspension, braking system, wheels or tyres, or to the electrical systems of the vehicle, that alters its performance, appearance or value from the manufacturer's specification. A modification that was declared and accepted by Saltmarsh Mutual, and for which any additional premium has been paid, is not affected by this exclusion. Where a modification was not declared, the policyholder should contact Saltmarsh Mutual promptly to discuss whether cover can be extended, since cover is not extended retrospectively.

## Business use

Cover under a private motor policy does not extend to the use of the vehicle for business purposes. Using the car for deliveries, or for visiting customers, clients or business premises, needs business use cover to be added to the policy before the journey is made. Where a vehicle is used for these purposes without the correct cover in place, any loss arising from that use may not be covered, even where the loss would otherwise fall within the policy.

Policyholders should review their use of the vehicle when their work or circumstances change, and should update their policy to reflect the actual use of the car. Saltmarsh Mutual does not accept responsibility for losses arising from undisclosed use.

## Relationship with other sections

These general exclusions apply across the motor wording and operate alongside the specific exclusions that appear under each cover level and rider. Where a rider such as accidental damage, legal expenses, home emergency or a courtesy car is held, the relevant rider wording sets out any additional exclusions for that benefit. If there is any inconsistency between a general exclusion and a rider exclusion, the wording that applies to the specific benefit in question will govern that benefit.

## Changes to this wording

This wording takes effect from 1 July 2026 and replaces any earlier general exclusions for motor cover. Saltmarsh Mutual may update this wording from time to time in line with changes in law, regulation or its own practices, and will notify policyholders of material changes in accordance with the policy conditions. Policyholders with questions about how an exclusion applies to their own circumstances should contact Saltmarsh Mutual before making a claim.
