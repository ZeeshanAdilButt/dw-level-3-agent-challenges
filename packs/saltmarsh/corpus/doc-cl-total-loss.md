# Total loss and written-off cars

## Purpose and scope

This document sets out how Saltmarsh Mutual decides whether a motor vehicle is a total loss, how that vehicle is valued, and what happens when a policyholder wishes to keep a vehicle that has been written off. It applies to claims under Motor Third Party and Motor Comprehensive policies, including claims where a rider such as accidental damage or a courtesy car is in place. It forms part of the claims handling rules that our staff follow from first notice of loss through to settlement, and it is published so that policyholders can understand how their claim will be assessed.

Saltmarsh Mutual is owned by its policyholders, and we aim to treat every claim fairly, consistently and transparently. Where this document refers to a vehicle, it means a car, van or motorcycle insured under one of our motor policies. Where a claim involves a home policy, the relevant rules are set out in our home claims guidance instead.

## Notifying us and the first steps

When a policyholder reports damage to their vehicle, we record the first notice of loss and arrange for the vehicle to be inspected. The inspection may take place at an approved repairer, at a storage location, or at the policyholder's home, depending on where the vehicle is and whether it can be safely driven. The purpose of the inspection is to establish the extent of the damage, the likely cost of repair, and whether the vehicle can be economically restored. Our assessors record photographs, the mileage at the time of the incident, and any evidence of earlier damage that may affect the assessment.

Policyholders should not authorise major repairs before the assessor has reviewed the vehicle, because work carried out before inspection can make it harder to determine whether the vehicle is a total loss. If a policyholder has already arranged repairs, they should tell us as soon as possible so that we can review the invoices and the condition of the vehicle.

## How a total loss is decided

A vehicle is a total loss when the repair cost is more than 60% of its market value. This test applies whether the damage comes from a collision, fire, theft followed by damage, flood or any other insured event. We compare the estimated cost of repairing the vehicle to a fair pre-loss value for the same vehicle, and where the repair cost exceeds the threshold we treat the vehicle as a total loss rather than repairing it.

The repair estimate includes parts, labour, paint, the cost of any specialist equipment needed to return the vehicle to a roadworthy condition, and reasonable allowances for hidden damage that is commonly found on vehicles of the same type and age. We do not include the cost of upgrades that the policyholder chose to make after the vehicle was first purchased. Where the estimate is disputed, we will review the evidence, including the policyholder's own independent estimate, before confirming our decision.

The threshold is applied to the repair cost compared with the market value immediately before the loss. It is not applied to the purchase price paid by the policyholder, and it does not depend on whether the policyholder has finished paying for the vehicle. If the repair cost is at or below the threshold, we will proceed with repair under the terms of the policy, subject to any excess that applies.

Some vehicles are unusual, imported, modified or classic, and their market value may be harder to establish. In these cases our assessors will look at the condition, specification and history of the vehicle and may seek specialist advice. We will explain in writing how the market value has been reached.

## How a total loss is valued

Once a vehicle has been confirmed as a total loss, we value it using trade guides on the date of the loss. Trade guides are recognised published valuation sources that record typical selling prices for vehicles by make, model, year, specification and mileage. We select the guide entry that most closely matches the vehicle, adjust for condition and mileage where the difference is material, and record the reasons for each adjustment.

The valuation is based on the market conditions at the date of the loss, not on the date the claim is settled. This means that a policyholder will not be disadvantaged if settlement takes time, and equally a market change after the loss will not affect the figure we agree. Where a trade guide does not contain a suitable entry, we will use comparable sales evidence from the same period and tell the policyholder what we have relied on.

Policyholders are entitled to see the valuation report and to challenge it. If a policyholder believes the valuation is too low, they may provide their own evidence, such as dealer quotations for similar vehicles, recent advertised prices, or details of options and extras that were not reflected in the guide. We will consider this evidence fairly and respond in writing. Where our assessment changes as a result, we will issue a revised settlement offer.

The settlement amount is the agreed market value at the date of the loss, less any applicable excess and any outstanding finance settlement that is owed to a lender. Where the policy includes a courtesy car rider, the courtesy car arrangement will continue for the period set out in the policy terms, subject to those terms, and it will not normally extend beyond the settlement of the total loss claim.

## Keeping a written-off vehicle

Some policyholders wish to keep a vehicle that has been declared a total loss, for example because they have an emotional attachment to it or because they intend to repair it themselves. This is possible in limited circumstances. A written-off vehicle may be kept only if Saltmarsh agrees, and only if the settlement is reduced by its salvage value.

Salvage value is the amount a vehicle would realise if it were sold in its damaged condition to a salvage buyer. We determine the salvage value through a recognised salvage market assessment at the date of the loss. Where a policyholder keeps the vehicle, the settlement offer is reduced by that salvage value, so that the policyholder receives the market value less the salvage value, minus any excess and finance settlement. We will confirm the figures in writing before any payment is made.

Keeping a written-off vehicle carries additional steps. The vehicle must be declared to the relevant vehicle licensing authority in accordance with the rules that apply in the jurisdiction where it is registered, and the policyholder is responsible for obtaining any necessary inspection or certification before the vehicle is used on the road again. We may ask to see evidence of this before we agree to the arrangement. Saltmarsh does not guarantee that a repaired vehicle will be accepted for road use, and we will not be responsible for any later cost or restriction that arises from the repair or from the vehicle's classification.

Where we do not agree to a request to keep a vehicle, the vehicle will be collected and disposed of through our salvage arrangements, and the settlement will be calculated on the full market value less any excess and finance settlement. We will explain our reasons in writing if a request is declined.

## Complaints and further information

If a policyholder disagrees with a total loss decision, the valuation, or the salvage arrangements, they should contact their claim handler in the first instance. If the matter cannot be resolved, it can be raised through our formal complaints process, and the policyholder will receive a written response explaining the outcome and any further options that are available to them.
