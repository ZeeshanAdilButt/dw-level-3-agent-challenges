# Clause 1: Buildings Cover

## Purpose and status

This clause sets out the wording for the buildings section of a Saltmarsh Mutual home policy. It is effective from 2026-07-01 and forms part of the policy schedule that the policyholder receives. Where this clause refers to the policyholder, it means the person named as the insured on the policy schedule. Where it refers to the Mutual, it means Saltmarsh Mutual, a home and motor insurer owned by its policyholders.

## Scope of cover

Clause 1 covers damage to the buildings by fire, lightning, explosion, storm, escape of water and theft. The buildings are the main structure of the home described in the schedule, together with any fixed outbuildings, garages, boundary walls and fixed fittings that form part of the structure. Cover applies while the buildings are at the address stated in the schedule and the policy is in force.

The cover is written to respond to sudden and identifiable events. Each of the perils listed above is intended to have its ordinary meaning, and the Mutual will assess a claim against the peril that caused the damage. Where damage results from more than one cause, the Mutual will consider the main cause of the loss when deciding whether this clause responds.

## How the sum insured applies

The policy schedule states a sum insured for the buildings. This is the amount the Mutual will pay, subject to the terms of this clause, for a total loss of the buildings. The sum insured should reflect the cost of rebuilding the buildings to their former condition, not their market value.

If the buildings sum insured is too low, any claim is reduced in the same proportion. In other words, the Mutual will compare the sum insured with the rebuilding cost of the buildings at the time of the loss. If the sum insured is less than that cost, the amount payable for a claim is reduced in the same proportion as the sum insured falls short of the full rebuilding cost. The reduction applies to the claim before any excess is deducted.

Policyholders are asked to review the buildings sum insured at each renewal and tell the Mutual about any building work, extension or change in construction that affects rebuilding cost.

## Fixed glass

Accidental breakage of fixed glass is covered only if the accidental damage rider is held. Fixed glass means glass that forms part of the buildings, such as windows, fixed panels and glazed doors, and that is not removable. Without the accidental damage rider, breakage of fixed glass by an accident is not covered under this clause, even where the cause of breakage is not otherwise excluded.

Where the policyholder holds the rider, accidental breakage of fixed glass is treated as damage to the buildings and is subject to the same sum insured, reduction and excess rules as other buildings claims. The rider does not extend cover for any peril other than accidental breakage.

## Steps to make a buildings claim

1. Tell the Mutual as soon as reasonably possible after the damage is discovered. Use the notification channel shown in the policy documents.
2. Take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, such as making the building secure and protecting it from the weather. Keep receipts for any emergency costs.
3. Keep damaged items or evidence until the Mutual has inspected them, unless it is unsafe to do so.
4. Provide any information the Mutual reasonably asks for, including photographs, a description of the cause and, for theft, any police reference.
5. Allow the Mutual to inspect the buildings and arrange an estimate of repair or rebuilding cost.
6. Agree the repair or rebuilding arrangements with the Mutual. The Mutual may appoint its own contractor or pay a cash settlement, as set out in the claim decision.
7. Accept the settlement or raise any concern through the Mutual's complaints process.

## Exclusions and excess

Clause 1 is subject to the exclusions and the excess table that apply to the policy. Exclusions set out circumstances in which no cover is provided, and they apply in addition to the terms of this clause. The excess table shows the amount the policyholder pays towards each claim, and it is deducted from the amount payable. Policyholders should read the exclusions and the excess table before relying on this clause.
