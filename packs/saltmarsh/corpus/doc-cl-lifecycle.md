# Claim statuses

## Purpose and scope

This document explains the claim statuses that Saltmarsh Mutual uses for home and motor claims. The statuses appear in the claim tracker and the claim tool, so policyholders and staff see the same wording. It applies to claims under Home Essentials, Home Plus, Motor Third Party and Motor Comprehensive, including claims made under the optional riders for accidental damage, legal expenses, home emergency and a courtesy car. It runs from first notice of loss through to settlement.

## The six claim statuses

There are six claim statuses. Each one describes where a claim stands at a given point. The table below lists them in the order a claim normally moves through them, with what each status means.

| Order | Status | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Received | The claim has been logged and not yet looked at by a handler. |
| 2 | Assessing | A handler is checking cover and the facts of the claim. |
| 3 | More information needed | The claim is paused until the policyholder sends something requested. |
| 4 | Approved | Saltmarsh has agreed to settle the claim and payment is being arranged. |
| 5 | Declined | Saltmarsh has decided the claim is not covered and has written to explain why. |
| 6 | Paid | The settlement has been sent. |

The status More information needed can occur at any point during assessment and may happen more than once on the same claim. Declined and Paid are final outcomes, and a claim will not move on from either of them.

## Normal order of progression

A claim normally moves from received to assessing, then to approved or declined, and then to paid. Where a claim is approved, it moves on to paid once the settlement has been sent. Where a claim is declined, the policyholder receives the written explanation and no payment follows.

If a claim needs further information, it moves into more information needed and waits there. When the requested information has been received, the claim returns to assessing and continues from that point. The claim does not lose its place in the process while it is paused.

## Acknowledgement and decision timing

Saltmarsh acknowledges a new claim within 2 working days of receiving it. This acknowledgement confirms that the claim has been logged and tells the policyholder what happens next. The claim will show the status received until a handler begins to review it.

Saltmarsh aims to decide a claim within 10 working days of receiving all the documents needed to make the decision. The clock for this target starts only once every requested document is in hand. Time spent in more information needed does not count toward this target, because the claim cannot be decided until the missing material arrives.

## How the statuses are shown

Policyholders can see the current status of their claim in the claim tracker. Handlers see the same status in the claim tool, which is also where the status changes are recorded. Each change of status is time stamped so that the history of a claim can be followed. Staff should not show a status to a policyholder that differs from the one recorded in the claim tool.

## Responsibilities

Claim handlers are responsible for moving a claim to the correct status as soon as the position changes. A claim should be moved to assessing once a handler begins checking cover and the facts, and to more information needed as soon as a request is sent to the policyholder. Handlers must make sure that the written decision accompanies any move to declined, and that a settlement is recorded before a claim is marked as paid.

Team leaders check that statuses are kept up to date and that claims are not left in one status without action. Any claim that has been in a single status for longer than expected should be reviewed by the team leader.

## Questions

Staff with questions about claim statuses should raise them with their team leader in the first instance. Questions from policyholders about the status of a claim should be answered using the wording in this document, so that the explanation given matches what the claim tracker shows.
