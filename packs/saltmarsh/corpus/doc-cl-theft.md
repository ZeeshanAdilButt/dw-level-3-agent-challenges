# Theft claims

This guide explains what to do after your home or vehicle has been stolen, how to report the incident to the police, what evidence Saltmarsh Mutual needs to assess a theft claim, and how the claim moves from first notice of loss to settlement. It applies to Home Essentials, Home Plus, Motor Third Party and Motor Comprehensive policies, and to any optional riders you have added to your policy.

## What to do straight after a theft

Your first priority is your safety and the safety of anyone in the property or vehicle. Once you are safe, avoid touching or moving anything at the scene, as the police may wish to examine it. If you can, make a note of what has been taken, where it was kept, and when you last saw it intact. Do not attempt to recover items yourself or confront anyone involved.

Contact Saltmarsh Mutual as soon as you reasonably can to open a claim. This first notice of loss allows us to record the incident, confirm the cover that applies to your policy, and explain the next steps. You will need your policy details and a clear account of what happened.

## Reporting to the police

Theft and malicious damage must be reported to the police within 24 hours of you discovering the incident. Reporting promptly helps the police investigate and is a condition of your claim being considered. If you are unable to attend a police station, most forces accept reports by telephone or online, depending on the nature of the crime.

Ask the police for a crime reference number and keep it safe. You will need this number to progress your claim, so please note it in your records along with the date and the name of the force that recorded it.

## Evidence we need

A theft claim needs the police crime reference number and proof of ownership for each item you are claiming for. Without these, we are unable to assess the claim fully, and settlement may be delayed while we ask for the missing information.

Proof of ownership can be a receipt, a bank statement or a dated photo of the item. A bank statement should show the payment for the item clearly, and a dated photo should show the item itself with the date it was taken. If you no longer have a receipt, a bank statement or a dated photo is usually sufficient.

For stolen items worth more than £1,000, a receipt or dated photo is needed in addition to your other evidence. This higher standard helps us verify high-value items quickly and fairly. If you are unsure which evidence to provide for a particular item, please contact us before submitting your claim and we will explain what is required.

## From claim to settlement

Once we have your crime reference number and proof of ownership, we will review the claim against your policy and any riders you hold. We may ask for further information, such as a list of all items claimed for or details of security measures in place at the time. We will keep you informed at each stage and explain any decision we reach.

If we agree to settle your claim, we will explain how the payment is calculated and when you can expect it. If you disagree with a decision, our complaints process is available to you, and the details are set out in your policy documents.
